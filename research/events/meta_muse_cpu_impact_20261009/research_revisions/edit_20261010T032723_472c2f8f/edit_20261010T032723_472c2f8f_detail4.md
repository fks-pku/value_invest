L136: ## How to read a plateau in an agent product
L137: 
L138: A flat curve can mean very different things.
L139: 
L140: A healthy plateau. The product retained most of the people it acquired, and new-user flow slowed after the launch spike. The base is stable and can grow with new features. This is common after a viral launch.
L141: 
L142: A leaky plateau. New users still arrive, but others leave at a similar rate, so the total stays flat. The total hides churn. This is a warning.
L143: A capacity plateau. Growth is limited by supply, such as invite codes or compute. Muse used invite codes and earlier reporting noted service strain, so this is plausible, though we cannot confirm it from the data. Our coverage of the cite76†invite-code rollout discusses the constraint.
L144: 
L145: A measurement plateau. The estimating firm's panel stopped capturing growth. Third-party data can plateau artificially.
L146: The data we have cannot distinguish these. Retention cohorts, meaning the share of day-one users still active on day 7 and day 30, would, and they are not public.
L147: ## What agent products need to earn daily use
L148: 
L149: A chat app earns daily use through habit: you open it when you have a question. An agent product has a harder job, because it competes with your time-saving alternatives and needs recurring tasks to justify opening. A few forces matter.
L171: For consumers, nothing here changes whether Muse is useful to you. For builders, the lesson is to treat spike metrics with suspicion, including your own. A launch can produce impressive numbers that say little about habit. And for anyone evaluating Meta's position against rivals such as OpenAI's Dots and SpaceXAI's Grok Bot, a fair comparison needs retention and usage-depth data, which none of them publish. Our cite83†five-agent comparison focuses on capabilities and risk for that reason.
L172: The honest summary is that a reported plateau at 2.5 to 3 million daily users is a modest warning sign, not a verdict. We will update this post if Meta or a named analytics vendor publishes better data.
