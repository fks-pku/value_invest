# Company Foundation Analysis Framework

Use this before applying any message-flow, catalyst, or FengHe analysis to an individual company. The goal is to understand what the company is, how it makes money, where power sits, and what can break the thesis.

This framework is a baseline analysis layer. It is not a buy/sell instruction and it should not predict short-term price moves.

## Eight Required Sections

1. Source and origin: founding context, original problem solved, founder or sponsor background, early customer or product wedge, and why the company exists.
2. Company history: major product, business, governance, financing, M&A, restructuring, listing, and capital-allocation milestones.
3. Current business: business model, segments, customers, demand drivers, pricing, unit economics, revenue quality, margin structure, and cash conversion.
4. Value chain position: suppliers, channels, customers, substitutes, bargaining power, cost pass-through, and where the company captures or loses economics.
5. Competitive landscape: market structure, peers, ranking, share trends, barriers to entry, differentiation, switching costs, and whether competition is intensifying.
6. Strategy analysis: mission, corporate strategy, competitive strategy, functional strategy, resource allocation, and whether strategy matches the company's position.
7. Organization, culture, and governance: leadership, incentives, board quality, ownership, control rights, culture, execution system, and governance risks.
8. Risk sweep: business, financial, accounting, legal, regulatory, customer concentration, technology, capital allocation, governance, valuation, and exogenous risks.

## Output Rules

- Every material claim needs an evidence ID or named data source.
- Separate facts, inferences, and judgments.
- Mark missing sections as gaps instead of filling them with generic language.
- Identify which foundation sections are stable, which are uncertain, and which require human review.
- Do not promote a thesis from foundation work alone; later message-flow analysis must test what is changing and what is already priced.
