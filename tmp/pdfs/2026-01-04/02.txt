Protein Boom: Be prepared…
The protein boom looks to be far from over, fuelled by health-
conscious consumer trends and the growing adoption of
GLP-1 treatments. We identify Protein Winners,
including Mowi (upgraded to OW), Danone, Novonesis,
Glanbia and in the US Tyson, JBS, Bellring, Hormel, Pilgrim's
Pride and Smithfield.
Consumers worldwide are increasingly prioritising protein in their diets, driving a boom in
sales of high-protein foods and beverages since the pandemic. The protein megatrend is still
in its ascent. If global intake shifts towards health-focused levels, we estimate that protein
demand could rise by 37%, equating to an incremental uplift of around US$650 billion in
market value. In collaboration with the Barclays Data Science team, we analyse the
penetration of protein-related products across different categories and which products/
brands are most popular among consumers. 
This note provides an expert cross-sector analysis of the 'protein boom' , identifying the
leading companies we see as best positioned to benefit from this megatrend. We call out: 1)
the structural drivers fuelling sustained growth, 2) the scale of investment and innovation
across the value chain, and 3) the clear outperformance of select 'Protein Winners' in both
volume and share price terms.

Equity Research: The protein boom looks set to
continue, driven by health trends and GLP-1 usage
The global appetite for protein shows no sign of slowing. Once a niche focus for fitness
enthusiasts, protein has now become a priority for households, driving robust growth across
multiple food and beverage categories. From high-protein yoghurts to ready-to-drink (RTD)
shakes, data shows that consumer demand is accelerating, fuelled by health and wellness
trends, the rise of GLP-1s and a growing emphasis on functional nutrition. As leading
brands invest in innovation and capacity, and new entrants vie for market share, the 'protein
boom' is reshaping the competitive landscape and opening up significant opportunities for
both established players and agile disruptors. We recently had a call with Jennifer Simpson
(Director, Chobani LLC, ex-Danone) and Barclays Fundamental & Sustainable Investing analysts,
discussing the sustainability of the strong trends in protein (link). Here we further demonstrate
why we think protein has not peaked and the winners in the category still have a growth
runway.
The global protein boom
Consumer demand for high-protein foods has accelerated in recent years, as health and
wellness comes into focus. The protein category, in particular dairy-based protein, has
experienced robust growth through 2024-25, most pronounced in the US – but now accelerating
in Europe. RTD protein shakes, high-protein yogurts and protein-enriched snacks have all seen
significant volume and household penetration gains. In a recent Numerator study, 78% of US
households said that they were paying attention to protein in their diets. Attention to
protein also increases with age, peaking among the baby boomers, with 83% stating that their
protein goals are important, which also extends internationally1 .
We estimate the global protein market at approximately US$1.7 trillion, encompassing
dairy, meat and all other protein sources. If consumption patterns shift towards health-focused
levels, we estimate that global protein demand could rise by 37% over the next five years.
Our analysis assumes optimal intake of 120g per adult per day, compared with the current
weighted average of 82g (UN), creating 38g of 'runway' . We derive this optimal intake
assumption based on current data, and factoring in the movement of lower-income countries

Health and wellness
Weight management, fitness trends and general health-consciousness are fuelling demand
for protein-rich options. Protein is a key ingredient in functional foods (foods that provide
health benefits beyond basic nutrition), with 23% of global consumers seeking protein-rich
foods, rising to 27% among exercisers. High-protein diets are among the five most popular

Policy pressure adds a second leg to price compression: MFN and Medicare IRA
negotiation. Beyond voluntary cash-pay discounts, US policy is pushing GLP-1 net prices lower.
The Trump administration’s new 'Most-Favored-Nation' (MFN) Executive Order directs HHS/CMS
to anchor US drug prices, including high-spend classes such as GLP-1s, to international
reference levels. The recent Trump-Novo-Lilly deal goes further for obesity: starting in mid-2026,
Medicare and Medicaid will pay around $245 per month for injectable GLP-1s and ~$149 for new
oral starters, while patient co-pays in Medicare will be capped at $50 per month. CMS has also
selected Ozempic/Wegovy among the drugs to receive negotiated Medicare prices starting in
2027 (IRA pricing at $275/month), with the administration projecting billions in annual savings.
Persistent use of compounded GLP-1s highlights the price sensitivity of patients who lack
insurance coverage. The significant use of compounded semaglutide during the shortage
period and continuing compounding pressure until today reflects in part the willingness of
patients to seek cheaper alternatives. GLP-1s were added to the FDA shortage list in 2022-24,
enabling pharmacies to produce generic copies and sell unapproved versions at steep
discounts. Despite the termination of compounding in May 2025, c1-1.5mn users are still using
compounded drugs, suggesting that at least a subset of consumers are price-sensitive. We
therefore expect lower GLP-1 prices to be more affordable for this subset of consumers, pointing
to increased usage.

Chris Stevens(iii) Barclays, UK  |  Luke Seelig(iii) Barclays, UK
One way to track increased consumer demand for high-protein foods and beverages is to see
which products at grocery stores contain 'protein' in the name and which products are selling

• The penetration of products with 'protein' in the name has increased over the last five

• By brand, Optimum Nutrition (a Glanbia brand) is the brand appearing most frequently in

Alex Sloane Barclays, UK

Why is it a Winner?
Mowi is the world’s largest producer of Atlantic salmon, giving it a dominant position in one of
the fastest-growing animal protein categories globally. We upgrade Mowi to OW from EW, and
raise our PT to DKK 260 from DKK 210. Please see link to our full upgrade note: Norwegian
Salmon: Protein plays with improved 2026 prospects. 
Salmon fillets deliver an average of about 20g of protein per 100g serving, with roughly 200
calories, making them a nutrient-dense option for high-quality protein. Salmon is sold primarily
as a fresh and unprocessed food that is clearly not an ultra-processed food (UPF) in the NOVA
classification system. If diets shift away from UPFs, this could see consumers increasing
consumption of salmon, which is high in protein and omega-3 fatty acids that are beneficial for
the heart and brain. The Dietary Guidelines for Americans (DGA) recommends eating seafood
twice a week and replacing high-fat meats with seafood in order to reduce intake of saturated
fat and sodium. Given that only 6% children and 20% of adults consume seafood twice per
week10 , there is a significant gap between the amount of seafood Americans currently eat and
the DGA recommendations. Mowi’s vertical integration – from feed to branded products –
delivers cost efficiency and margin resilience, giving it a competitive edge as demand for
salmon protein accelerates. We turn more constructive on the Salmon space, reflecting what we
see as improved 2026 fundamentals and increasing structural appeal.
What are the risks?
Biological challenges such as sea lice and disease outbreaks could disrupt volumes and inflate
costs for Mowi. Exposure to volatile salmon prices and feed inflation threatens margin stability.
Regulatory and environmental scrutiny around open-net farming may drive compliance costs or
restrict operations, and geopolitical trade risks add uncertainty to global distribution. Longer
term, competition from alternative proteins and plant-based seafood could erode category

Danone
Warren Ackerman Barclays, UK
Rating: OW, PT: EUR 85.00
Why is it a Winner?
Danone is a global leader in food and beverages, operating across essential dairy and plant-
based products, waters and specialised nutrition. Danone has established itself as a leader in
the protein category through decades of R&D in protein and has high-protein sales that we
estimate will approach €2bn by the end of 2026. The company’s high-protein platforms such as
Oikos and YoPro are delivering double-digit growth and have been rolled out in more than 30
countries. 
The company leverages its decades of nutritional science to target emerging health trends, such
as launching Oikos Fusion for GLP-1 medication users, while expanding its high-protein
footprint into Europe, Japan and Latin America, where penetration remains low. Critically,
Danone’s health-focused portfolio aligns with rising consumer demand for functional benefits
in fitness, weight management and gut health, driving volume/mix growth even in mature
markets. The company is expanding manufacturing capacity specifically for high-protein
products, acknowledging current supply constraints, but this pressure should ease as new
capacity comes on stream in 2026. High protein categories account for 50% of the EDP market in
the US, but less than a quarter of the European EDP market, on our estimates, which shows the
potential for further growth in Europe.
What are the risks?
While Danone is seeing booming high-protein demand, because of shortage of supply it is
directing some of its capacity away from its core ranges in the US, which is impacting its market
shares. Danone is also undertaking a big push into the ambient protein shake market with its
Oikos brand and it is not clear how big a growth driver this might be, as we are still awaiting
repeat purchase data. Clearly, companies are vying for the growth potential in the high-protein
space and Danone is facing intense competition from competitors such as Chobani. In Europe,
the high-protein market is also quite crowded and there is the risk that private-label
competition also increases as the market grows and household penetration surges.

Novonesis
Alex Sloane Barclays, UK
Rating: OW, PT: DKK 470.00
Why is it a Winner?
Novonesis has established a leading position in protein solutions through its brand-agnostic
Food & Beverages biosolutions platform, serving a broad range of industry players.
Approximately 60% of this platform’s focus is on dairy applications, which account for c20% of
the overall group. The company offers a broad portfolio of ingredient solutions, specialising in
cultures, enzymes and probiotics that are critical in producing high-protein yoghurt and
cheese – and key inputs for manufacturers to enhance yield, quality, digestibility and
sustainability. Novonesis’ dairy portfolio is delivering standout double-digit underlying growth
that is broad-based across high-protein yoghurt, but also boosted by increased penetration of
Direct Vat Set (DVS) cultures in cheese (with global conversion still at only ~60%, offering
significant headroom) and productivity/yield-enhancing solutions.
On its recent results call, the company highlighted strong momentum in both developed and
emerging markets, with China cited as a long-term opportunity for cheese. Cottage cheese
demand is also growing strongly on the back of high-protein diet trends. Management indicated
that it is “excited about dairy growth over the next several years” , which we think partly reflects
significant investments by customers in new high-protein dairy capacity – Chobani’s $500
million upgrade in Twin Falls, Idaho (adding 50% capacity) and a new $1.2 billion plant in Rome,
NY, as well as Danone’s ongoing expansion at its Minster, Ohio facility to meet surging demand.
At our Boston conference Novonesis highlighted that it can typically sell up to 3x as much into
high-protein yoghurts as conventional yoghurt formats, driven by the need for more DVS
cultures but also enzymes that enhance taste and texture as well as selling in biosolutions that
enhance shelf life naturally through bioprotection.
Longer term, Novonesis is developing enzyme solutions to maximise protein content in whey
by-product streams from cheese customers. The commercial appeal of such solutions is clear as
demand for whey for high-protein claims across CPG brands continues to rise globally. If
Novonesis can replicate the success it has had in maximising co-product value streams for
bioenergy customers with its cheese customers, the incremental growth opportunity could be
very attractive. Additionally, through its Human Health division the company has developed an
Advanced Protein Solutions business that uses precision fermentation to develop a wide range
of plant-based protein solutions.
What are the risks?
The key risk to Novonesis sustaining top-line growth in F&B biosolutions is reduced end-market
demand for yoghurt and cheese. Key competitors include IFF (Danisco) and DSFIR – we believe
the former has been something of a market share donor to NSISB over the past decade as
disruption associated with major M&A has led to some share loss. In our view, with a credible
and stable management team now in situ, it is possible that IFF emerges as a stronger
competitor and recovers some market share in Dairy biosolutions over the next five years. In
addition, there could be new sources of competition emerging in China. A further risk is that
Novonesis' cultures production is relatively concentrated in Denmark – should there be a
quality incident, it could disrupt sales. Exports of cultures from Denmark also create some FX
transactional exposure, particularly vs. USD. The company is expanding its Milwaukee cultures
production to mitigate this risk, but this new capacity will not come on stream until H2 26.

Consumer interest in poultry has continually risen due to the broader demand for protein as
well as a greater focus on healthier diets, where chicken presents lower-calorie and fat content
than red meat counterparts.
Additionally, as the price of beef has risen to record highs, the relatively affordability of poultry
has increased and has driven consumers towards the category. PPC management noted on its
3Q25 that, despite broadly declining foodservice traffic, volumes for chicken continue to
improve as customers seek more price-conscious options. The company’s diverse portfolio of
different bird sizes allows it to reach customers at both the retail and restaurant level,
responding to specific key customer needs as consumer preferences evolve. Looking ahead,
PPC will continue to expand through a series of capital projects, including expansions in Mexico
and a new prepared foods facility in the US. These new facilities allow PPC to better tap into the
higher-margin, more stable prepared foods and branded products categories, bringing potential
to improve earnings stability, profitability and market share.
What are the risks?
A reversal of the chicken cycle would be detrimental to margins, as oversupply typically drives
lower pricing. Higher feed costs would also cut into profitability, as would events such as
disease.

Why is it a Winner?
BRBR is a pure-play protein company focused on the convenient nutrition category with its

market share, and the company continues to benefit from category tailwinds as the RTD protein
shake category grew +15% YOY in the quarter and closer to +18% YOY over the past 12 months.
The company’s more premium Dymatize ready-to-mix (RTM) powder product is also well-
positioned in an RTM protein powder category that’s grown ~+10% YOY over the past 12
months.

RTD shakes by some +20%+ YoY across the food, drug, mass and e-commerce channels in FY26

Credit Research: Protein Winner – Boparan
Karine Elias(i) Barclays Bank, DIFC
Boparan holds the number one position in the UK poultry market with an estimated 28.1%
market share in terms of volume, and the second-largest position in the ready meals market,

and added-value products, which are then marketed and sold to consumers primarily via major
retailers and foodservice manufacturers.
Boparan has nine poultry processing and manufacturing sites in the UK and its integrated
British supply chain in the poultry segment helps position the company to grow and take
advantage of the increased demand for British-sourced chicken. Poultry sales have continued to
exceed management’s expectations and margins have benefited from the increased proportion
of welfare poultry sales. During the Q1 26 results (reported on 16 December), management
commented that supply remains tight following the transition to higher-welfare poultry and
demand remains high, resulting in better carcass balance and a high proportion of sales to retail

management explained that over 80% of poultry sales and 89% of farm space meet the higher
welfare standard. The company is implementing a capital plan for the next 10 years, totalling
nearly £1bn, to add capacity, expand capability and drive further efficiencies through
automation, including: three new poultry factories; deep chill capacity; a new kill line; and
automatic cutting and deboning machines. We have a Market Weight rating on BOPRLN

Equity Research: To Summarise...
The protein megatrend is still in its ascent. We estimate the global protein market at ~US$1.7
trillion today across dairy, meat and alternative sources. As consumption patterns shift toward
health-focused intake, the data indicate substantial runway potential. If global intake shifts
towards health-focused levels, we estimate that protein demand could rise by 37%, equating
to an incremental uplift of around US$650 billion in market value. 
In this scenario, our analysis points to a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 6.6% for
global protein demand over the next five years, as health trends and GLP-1 adoption
accelerate. This growth is evident across categories: protein-rich snacks are growing at 1.3x the
rate of the overall snacks market; RTD protein shakes and high-protein yoghurts are seeing
significant volume and household penetration gains; and plant-based proteins are expanding at
8-10%+ CAGRs in key markets. At the same time, natural protein sources such as eggs and meat
continue to see robust demand. Companies that innovate early and diversify into high-protein
product lines are capturing incremental sales and positioning for long-term growth as health
and wellness priorities accelerate globally. 
Structural tailwinds are intensifying. GLP-1 adoption is broadening beyond diabetes into
weight management, pushing consumers to prioritise protein to preserve lean mass. We
expect pricing normalisation and expanded coverage to extend duration on therapy and widen
the user base, amplifying everyday demand for protein-rich foods. In parallel, supply is scaling:
manufacturers have committed multi-billion dollar capex to unlock constrained capacity in
high-protein dairy and adjacent formats. These investments should ease bottlenecks and
support category innovation from 2026 onward.
Against this backdrop, our Protein Winners Mowi (upgraded to OW), Danone, Novonesis,
Glanbia, Tyson Foods, JBS, Hormel Foods, Pilgrim’s Pride, Smithfield Foods and BellRing
Brands have already outperformed peers in volume and share-price basket terms, reflecting
advantaged exposure to the most attractive growth nodes and capabilities in R&D, capacity and
brand execution. In our view, while competition will intensify and growth rates may normalise
quarter to quarter, consumers have not reached 'peak protein' yet and the secular trend of
protein as a daily, functional and enjoyable part of mainstream diets remains under-
appreciated.
