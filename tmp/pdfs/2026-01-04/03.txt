The Forest Report - 3Q25 Edition
Early signs for headwinds ahead as electronics and auto
trade-in subsidies and tax incentives have started to lap; AI
capex in Q3 down qoq and below expectations due to chip
supplies or perhaps management's rational minds.
Trade-in subsidy and take rate increases lapped; ecommerce players need new growth
engines: BABA (OW) sustained solid growth momentum with CMR up 10% yoy in Sep-Q, but we
expect 4Q to decelerate as take rate tailwinds fade. We remind investors that BABA introduced
or re-introduced 0.6% of GMV platform fees in early Sep 2024. Stabilizing/gaining market share
remains its focus, and quick commerce will generate synergies with its core ecommerce. The
lapping effect of trade-in benefits is more pronounced at JD (OW), seeing only 5% yoy growth in
elec. & appliances, down from teens to over 20% yoy growth in the previous three quarters, and
we expect flattish yoy in 4Q. On the other hand, JD’s general merchandise and marketplace
continued to accelerate, supporting retail topline and margin expansion; however, profitability
was offset by investments in new initiatives (mainly food delivery). Similarly, Xiaomi (OW) IoT
(mostly home appliances) growth slowed to 6% yoy, down from 45% to 59% yoy growth in the
previous three quarters. And the company continues to aim to unlock upside through high-end
products and overseas expansion. PDD (OW), while less exposed to trade-in programs, faces
intense competition that pressured online marketing growth to 8% yoy (a proxy for domestic
revenue), as the company ramps up investments in merchants and consumers, negatively
impacting its take rate.
Food delivery loss likely peaked in 3Q, yet competition remains elevated and Meituan's
profitability may never go back to its past peaks in our view: In Sep-Q, we estimate BABA's
quick commerce loss at -RMB 36.5bn, Meituan's (UW) core local commerce (CLC) investment at
-RMB 28.6bn (3Q loss vs profit a year ago), and JD's loss slightly below 2Q’s -RMB10-13bn. On
competitive dynamics, BABA maintained order market share while GMV share edged up driven
by higher AOV, while halving UE loss in Oct vs Jul and Aug. Meituan led GMV market share in
mid-to-high AOV orders (>RMB 15), and saw order share rebound a bit in Nov. JD's order volume
remained relatively stable. All major players expect food delivery losses to narrow in 4Q driven
by UE improvement and lower subsidies - industry subsidies trended down in Oct & Nov vs
summer peak, and cooled further post Double 11 Shopping festival. We think intense
competition persists across food and grocery shopping, particularly as Meituan plans to
increase investment in grocery to enhance its leadership. Overall, with BABA's renewed focus in
the food delivery area and given it is less of a priority for BABA to generate any meaningful
profits from this segment when the company can monetize through higher customer
engagement and higher transaction frequency on its regular ecommerce platforms, we consider
Meituan's margin profile has been structurally changed (lowered) and the mid 20s operating
margin is unlikely to return in the foreseeable future.

Ecommerce
BABA: CMR maintained solid growth momentum at 10% yoy, but BABA expected CMR growth in
4Q to decelerate as take rate drivers (0.6% software service fee and ad tool QZT) lap. It remains
focused on stabilizing/gaining market share in the mid-to-long term. Quick commerce synergies
were mainly on groceries, healthcare, and supermarket products, while BABA is enhancing
cross-selling with Tmall brands. Per our estimate, when excluding quick commerce loss of RMB
36.5bn, China ecommerce EBITA margin remained solid at 42.9% in Sep-Q.
JD: General merchandise and marketplace again accelerated revenue growth to 19%/24% yoy,
respectively, driving retail growth and margin expansion. However, electronics & appliances
growth slowed significantly to 5% yoy as trade-in programs started to lap, and we expect 4Q
growth to decelerate further to flattish yoy due to tough comp as 4Q will have full quarter
lapping effect and 3Q only had partial quarter lapping. As such, we expect group/retail growth
slows to 11%/9% in 4Q from 15%/11% in 3Q. JD retail recorded ~RMB15bn operating income in
3Q yet offset by the new initiatives loss (mainly from food deliveries). We expect group adj. OPM
to stay around zero in 4Q.
PDD: Online marketing revenue (a proxy for its China business) decelerated for seven
consecutive quarters to 8% yoy in 3Q, due to intense competition and the company’s subsidy
program to support merchants thus lowering the take rate. We expect PDD's China domestic
business to continue to see modest growth in the coming quarters, and its margins remain
under pressure due to competition and subsidies. On the other hand, we estimate TEMU GMV
likely re-accelerated in 3Q as tariff tension eased a bit. But profits from PDD's domestic business
are critical in funding TEMU’s global expansion.
Kuaishou: GMV decelerated to 15% yoy, in line with our expectation driven by purchase
frequency, while number of transacting customers remained stable. Ecommerce revenue likely
grew 34% yoy per our estimate, as KS increased commissions from some KOLs to reinvest in the
ecosystem. But management noted that this high take rate is not sustainable and will gradually
smooth out. We expect GMV to further decelerate to 13% yoy in 4Q.

Food Delivery
BABA: Instant commerce maintained order market share, while GMV share increased driven by
higher AOV, which rose DD in November vs August. Order mix has become healthier, with non-
beverage orders representing over 75% of total orders. More encouragingly, unit losses in
October halved vs July and August partly driven by scale effect that boosted logistics efficiency.
As such, we estimate losses to narrow to ~RMB 25bn in 4Q from the peak of RMB 36.5bn in 3Q.
BABA reiterated its focus remains on market share while improving UE.
Meituan: Amid intense competition, Meituan led mid-to-high AOV basket food GTV market
share (orders over RMB 15), and sustained leadership in instashopping. However, CLC operating
income turned negative for the first time since 2021 to -RMB 14bn with opm of -21%, down over
-RMB28bn vs a year ago due to increased subsidies. It expects food delivery losses to narrow in
4Q as industry subsidies started to trend lower in October and November. However,
management anticipates instashopping losses to expand qoq due to investments to enhance
market leadership. Furthermore, in-store/hotel/travel margin may also trend lower as peers are
increasing investments. Meanwhile, the accelerated international expansion will increase losses
for its new initiatives segment. Overall, we expect 4Q group opm to stay low at -20% (vs 3Q’s
-21%).
JD: JD food delivery losses narrowed vs 2Q’s approx. -RMB 10-13bn level driven by improved
UE, and JD expected losses to further narrow in 4Q. JD expects this food business to be self-
sufficient eventually. It reiterated no intention of price war, and its focus is on high-quality
merchants. Average daily orders were relatively stable at 25mn during 11.11 and 618 shopping
festivals. Encouragingly, synergy to retail business is evident. JD shared that conversion rate of
new users attracted by food to purchase retail goods is improving month over month, with
supermarket, electronics, and Jingxi categories benefiting the most from cross selling.

Digital Ads
Tencent: Ad revenue grew 21% yoy with broad gains across major categories. Around half of
this growth came from higher eCPM driven by AI-powered ad technology and increased closed-
loop ad demand, with the other half due to higher ad impressions resulting from greater user
engagement (higher DAU and time spent) and increased ad load across video accounts, mini
programs, and Weixin search. AI advancements continue to drive ad business growth, notably
by boosting commercial queries and click-through rates in Weixin search, where LLMs have
improved ads relevance and optimized matching to user queries. In terms of Mini Shops, it aims
to benchmark with ad GMV penetration level in eCommerce marketplace in China. Looking into
2026, management expects ad growth momentum to continue, supported by modest
improvements in consumer spending and further AI enhancements. Looking ahead, we expect
Tencent’s ads revenue to continue to grow high teens to 20% yoy in the next few quarters.
Kuaishou: Ad revenue growth accelerated to 14% yoy in 3Q, while domestic growth was higher
at 16% yoy, driven by increases in eCPM and ad load. External ad contributed roughly half of the
revenue and delivered low teens % yoy revenue growth, driven by lifestyle services (e.g. in-store
services, financials, education, and auto) and content consumption sectors (e.g. short plays,
novels, and mini-games). Closed-loop (internal) ad revenue increased mid-teens % yoy, while
KS continues to increase ad penetration in shelf-based ecommerce and improve the ROI bidding
recommendation tools to increase sales conversion. Looking ahead, we expect KS’s ads revenue
to grow low teens in the next couple of quarters.
BILI: Ad business continued to show strong growth in 3Q25, with performance ads experiencing
particularly robust momentum and now accounting for around half of total ad revenue. The
expansion was fueled by higher DAUs, increased ad load (up ~1ppt yoy per quarter), and better
eCPM from improved ROI. Management believes the platform’s increasingly mature user base
makes it more attractive to marketers, especially brands seeking to reach affluent, online-savvy
consumers. The top five advertising verticals for the quarter were games, internet services,
digital products & home appliances, ecommerce, and automobiles. During the 11.11 Shopping
Festival, BILI’s ad revenue jumped 30% yoy, and the number of advertisers more than doubled.
Looking ahead, we expect BILI’s ads revenue to grow at roughly 20% yoy in coming quarters.
BIDU: BIDU’s ad revenue were -18% yoy in 3Q, though this was a better result than anticipated,
with slight qoq improvement in 4Q possible. Notably, AI-native marketing services (AI ad
revenue) surged to RMB 2.8 billion, up 262% yoy, now contributing 18% of total ad revenue
compared to 4% a year ago. The majority of AI ad revenue comes from AI ads from AI agents,
with the remainder mostly from digital humans. By the end of October, about 70% of mobile
search result pages included AI search results, up from 50% at the end of Q2, which has driven
greater user engagement and higher usage retention. Looking ahead, we expect BIDU’s ads
revenue to continue to decline yoy but the rate of the decline may be less than in 3Q.

AI and Cloud
BABA: Cloud revenue growth accelerated to 34% yoy, +5.1% vs our estimate, reaching nearly
RMB 40bn this quarter driven by strong AI demand. Management expects this growth
momentum to continue in the coming quarters with relatively stable margins. It believes AI
bubble is unlikely within the next two to three years, as it is seeing enterprises are accelerating
adoption of AI while AI supplies (including GPUs, data centers, etc) may need to ramp up for at
least two to three years in order to catch up to the strong demand. As a result, its three-year
capex plan of RMB 380bn (~$54bn) announced earlier this year may ultimately prove to be too
conservative (low). However, as the chip shortage remains a key constraint, its Sep-Q capex was
actually lower than Jun-Q.
Tencent: Tencent retained its AI focus on empowering its own ad and gaming business, and is
actively integrating AI features to Weixin and other applications. Similar to BABA, Tencent’s
operating capex in 3Q trended lower vs 2Q due to chip supply shortage. And Tencent reduced its
FY25 capex forecast to be lower than originally guided low-teens % of revenue, but still higher
than FY24. Management stated that its GPUs are mostly only sufficient for internal uses, but not
enough for it to provide significant external AI cloud services (though external AI cloud services
revenue still grew notably yoy in Q3).
BIDU: Baidu AI cloud infrastructure revenue increased 33% yoy driven by subscription-based
revenue from AI infrastructure. Also, AI has boosted ad revenue growth, with AI ad contributing
18% of total ad revenue (vs 4% a year ago) mainly through AI agents and digital humans. We
expect cloud revenue to decelerate in 4Q to low teens due to fluctuations in project-based
enterprise revenue, which BIDU is trying to stabilize over time.
Kuaishou: Kling revenue increased to RMB 300mn in 3Q, and KS raised FY25 revenue guidance
to $140mn, implying another RMB 300mn revenue in 4Q. We note that competition in video AI
apps has intensified as more global peers are seeking breakthroughs in video-generation field
and have launched their video AI apps in recent months often free of charge to users. We
estimate KS’s FY25 capex to be about RMB 14bn (up DD yoy), but still far below global peers.

Electric Vehicles
Xiaomi: Xiaomi (OW) delivered 108.8k EVs in 3Q25, up 34% qoq, with ASP rising to RMB 260k
due to the SU7 Ultra and YU7. Annual EV deliveries has already surpassed 350k units, meeting
its prior target. Gross margin dipped slightly qoq to 25.5% from a lower SU7 Ultra mix. A 5% EV
purchase tax (up to RMB 15k per vehicle) starts in 2026, but Xiaomi is looking to rebate the
increased tax for its EVs if consumers place firm orders by November 30 even if the deliveries
happen in 2026, and this tax rebate may pressure margins next year. The EV and AI segment
turned profitable for the 1st time in 3Q, within six quarters of EV launch, and continued
profitability is expected from scaling, YU7 ramp-up, and operational improvements. Looking
ahead, we expect Xiaomi to deliver 135k units in 4Q25 and 401k units for 2025.
