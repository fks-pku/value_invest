2026 Outlook: Consumer Staples – Quality Over Complexity
If we take Deutsche Bank's House View  on 2026 as given (a landscape 
characterized by a broadly positive yet volatile "risk-on" macro environment driven 
by AI advancements, Fed rate cuts, and growing trade certainty), the Consumer 
Staples sector faces a challenging setup over the year ahead. Such a backdrop 
naturally places traditionally defensive sectors like Staples out of favor, yet even 
more so given that historically consistent/stable fundamental trends across CPG 
have remained highly elusive over the past ~18 months of net downward revisions. 
In our view, this context demands a heightened focus on fundamental quality and 
simplicity when it comes to CPG stock picking, as investors without a natural 
"need" to own Staples are likely to be increasingly discerning. Moreover, should 
such a "need" to own Staples unexpectedly arise, quality and simplicity are likely to 
be the first ports of call.
Our strategic approach to 2026 is therefore fairly selective, biased towards scale, 
and, crucially, emphasizes quality over complexity. We remain most constructive 
on Non-Alcoholic Beverages and Beauty/Personal Care, as leading franchises there 
demonstrate resilient brand loyalty, sophisticated revenue growth management, 
and an ability to navigate consumer trends effectively through targeted innovation 
and varied value offerings—hallmarks of inherent quality. Conversely, our caution 
extends to Packaged Food and Alcoholic Beverages, where profound long-cycle (if 
not structural) headwinds, including evolving health and wellness trends and the 
impact of GLP-1 adoption, introduce greater complexity and market uncertainty.
This analytical lens guides our specific stock selections. We maintain Buy ratings 
on KO, MNST, EL, PG, CHD, and PEP, and today upgrade MKC to join their ranks—
all strong historical franchises with either clear relative momentum ( KO, MNST, 
MKC) or elevated prospects for sequential improvement (EL, PG, CHD, PEP) over 
the course of CY26, in our view. We conversely remain more cautious on names in 
the midst of complex/large-scale M&A (e.g., KMB/KVUE, KHC), and thus 
downgrade KDP to Hold amidst integration demands, potential coffee questions, 
rising CSD/energy competition, and following a +10% run over the past month. We 
also migrate to more of a "wait-and-see" approach on most of our more volatile 
SMID cap coverage (at least until such time as clearer catalysts present 
themselves), prompting downgrades to Hold at FRPT and BRBR (each up +13% 
and +18%, respectively over the past month).

A Challenging Setup for Staples in 2026
As we look ahead to 2026, Deutsche Bank's House View—aptly titled "Anything but 
dull"—paints a macro picture that is volatile but broadly positive, suggesting a 
challenging setup for defensive sectors like Consumer Staples. The dominant 
economic theme is expected to be the transformative potential of AI, which, 
coupled with ongoing Fed rate cuts and (hopefully) greater certainty on the trade 
front, is expected to drive a re-acceleration in US growth to +2.4%. This optimism 
is underscored by our US equity strategist’s year-end S&P 500 target of 8,000. 
While global growth is forecast to remain steady at +3.2%, the regional drivers are 
shifting, with a notable rebound in Germany (+1.5% growth) acting as a key tailwind 
for the European economy. This broadly "risk-on" backdrop, characterized by a 
cyclical upswing and positive market sentiment, likely positions our sector for 
continued relative underperformance,1 forcing a heightened focus on company-
specific fundamentals and self-help initiatives to drive shareholder value in the year 
ahead. That said, with relative valuations at/near 20-year lows, our negativity is 
partially tempered by the back door possibility that even marginal disappointment 
across tech or the broader market/economy could force capital to view Staples 
stocks more favorably (especially if coupled by any sequential improvement in 
Staples fundamentals).

Such a backdrop forces us to be selective and biased broadly towards scale, quality, 
and simplicity across the group—i.e., as macro dynamics that have historically 
buoyed Staples during periods of uncertainty are poised to abate, and as sector-
specific complexities/headwinds are likely to remain. From a bottom-up 
perspective, the primary challenge for the group in 2026, in our view, will likely be 
navigating an environment where the consumer, while benefiting from wage 
growth and tax cuts, remains discerning and value-conscious after years of high 
inflation (especially the lower income consumer, to which Staples companies over-
index). This dynamic will continue to result in a difficult operating environment 
where companies must "earn" net price through tangible innovation and/or 
superior value propositions (or risk trade down/trade out to private label/discount 
brands)—a continuation of 2025 dynamics, and a stark contrast to the broad-based 
pricing power the industry enjoyed in 2022-2023. Moreover, headwinds to birth 
rates, immigration, household formation, and SNAP benefits serve as further 
constraints on consumption, as do certain Health & Wellness trends/GLP-1 
adoption in many traditionally mainstream Food/Beverage categories (at the same 
time that regulatory oversight challenges legacy formulations). Consequently, as 
the sector overall likely continues to struggle for alpha, relative stock selection will 
remain paramount.
Relative Opportunity/Risks Across Subsectors
Diving deeper into CPG subsectors, we see a clear hierarchy of opportunity heading 
into 2026. We remain most fundamentally constructive  on Non-Alcoholic 
Beverages and Beauty/Personal Care (even if valuations already reflect a similar 
market bias). Leading Non-Alcoholic Beverage companies, with sophisticated 
revenue growth management capabilities and resilient brand loyalty, appear best 
equipped to navigate the nuanced consumer landscape through price-pack 
architecture and targeted innovation. Similarly, Beauty & Personal Care should 
benefit from both premiumization trends among more affluent consumers and a 
defensive, value-seeking orientation among mass market cohorts, allowing well-
positioned players to capture growth at both ends of the value spectrum. The 
ongoing rebound in travel and social occasions should also provide a durable 
tailwind for these more experiential and consumer-facing categories.
Conversely, we are more cautious on Packaged Food and Alcoholic Beverages. 
While arguably priced accordingly, these subsectors face more profound structural 
headwinds, including aforementioned shifts toward health and wellness that we 
view as at least a long-cycle trend, if not fully secular. While pockets of growth do 
exist (e.g., protein), we see greater risks competition and volume pressure, and thus 
a need for elevated promotional spending to maintain market share. Moreover, we 
see related inclinations to rethink existing portfolios (i.e., to enhance consumer 
alignment) creating heightened capital allocation risks for those most 
disadvantaged.
Finally, we remain more neutral on traditional Household Care and/or Disposable 
Paper Products . While such categories offer defensive stability and potential 
platforms for differentiated innovation, they generally also carry higher private label 
exposure and lack widespread catalysts for significant growth. Moreover, 
companies could face margin pressure if they are forced to pass lower pricing 
(whether prompted by easing commodity costs or otherwise) back to retailers and 
consumers.

Specific Stock Positioning Preferences
In September, heading into the CY3Q25 reporting season, we published a sector-
wide note entitled, "US CPG: Current State of Staples – "Not So Stapley" (link). We 
noted then that since the middle of 2023 (and again here in 2025), stock 
performance of Food, Beverage, and HPC names, in aggregate, has generally 
languished—materially trailing the broader market, as well as other Staples 
subsectors such as Tobacco and Staples Retailing. CPG multiples have 
compressed, and Food, Beverage, and HPC stocks, as mentioned above, now trade 
at near 20-year lows relative to the S&P 500, with Consumer Staples stocks making 
up less than 6% of the S&P 500 (their lowest weighting since the early-2000s). While 
much of this underperformance can be ascribed to strength in other areas of the 
market (most notably tech), fundamental challenges in CPG are also to blame.
Over the past 12+ months, and right up into December, we have observed 
persistent pressure on consensus (and DBe) estimates across EPS, organic growth, 
and gross margin, resulting in a string of downward revisions. FY25/26 organic 
growth expectations across the group have slowed from over +3% entering the year 
to now no better than ~+1.0%, with higher operating costs also challenging 
profitability. Given the soft state of consumer sentiment /confidence (especially 
among lower income cohorts), exacerbated by heightened geopolitical and 
economic uncertainty, we don't see these conditions materially improving in the 
year ahead. As we look forward, our recommendations are increasingly biased 
towards larger-cap quality, narrative simplicity, and sound or improving execution, 
with diminished exposure to complex corporate actions, governance questions, or 
pending transactions.
Consequently, this updated thinking leads to our decision to downgrade KDP to 
Hold while upgrading MKC to Buy. We also downgrade FRPT and BRBR each to 
Hold following recent strength and what we see as relatively binary risk from here 
on top-line/consumption improvements in early-2026.
Further, we reiterate our Buy ratings on KO, MNST, EL, PG, CHD, PEP, MKC, as well 
as NOMD (largely on valuation and a weak-dollar bias) and DOLE (again on 
valuation alongside easing supply conditions).

Building on Solid Fundamental Momentum – KO, MNST, EL
As mentioned above, we remain fundamentally constructive on non-alcoholic 
beverages (best represented by KO and MNST) and prestige beauty (best 
represented by EL), even if valuations already reflect a sympathetic market bias.
n KO (Buy, Price Target $82) – At KO, there is no question that operating 
conditions are dynamic and therefore difficult to manage globally, and a 
pending CEO transition  introduces undeniable uncertainty (even if the 
transition has been long in the company's plans, as we believe it has). 
However, absolute performance from KO (and its bottling partners) remains 
best-in-class relative to large-cap CPG peers, and a weaker-dollar, ramping 
fairlife capacity in the US, and improving FCF should all be YOY positives for 
KO in FY26. If the DB House View is correct, we also see KO relatively well-
positioned among CPG names to benefit from a cyclical upswing in demand 
given potentially favorable implications for KO's profitable away-from-
home and immediate consumption channels.
n MNST (Buy, Price Target $84) – We continue to see strong prospects for 
MNST in the year ahead, with recent category tailwinds likely to continue 
(given consumer preferences for energy functionality, industry/ MNST 
innovation around zero sugar, and high relative pricing for coffee). For 
MNST specifically, assuming sound execution in cooperation with Coke 
System bottlers, we see continued gains internationally, with promising US 
momentum building behind a strong 2026 innovation slate (as articulated 
at NACS and a recent Investor Day). Moreover, despite pockets of cost 
pressure and reinvestment plans, margin/earnings growth should 
ultimately persevere (benefitting in part from a seemingly far more 
advanced approach to pricing/RGM). We raise our price target to $84 (from 
$77), underpinned by better growth prospects both in the US and overseas, 
margin benefits from supply chain efficiencies and RGM initiatives, and a 
slightly lower WACC (-25 bps).
n EL (Buy, Price Target $124) – Given still-cautious but generally favorable 
commentary about beauty industry demand trajectories recently (both in 
the US and China) from EL and peers alike, we remain optimistic about 
building momentum in FY2Q 2  and into FY2H/CY26+ for EL—aided by 
greater brand support, active channel diversification efforts, and increasing 
innovation contribution (all key pillars of new management's Beauty 
Reimagined strategy). With such sustained progress on the top line should, 
in turn, come progressive progress on profitability/margins and FCF, further 
enabled by Profit Recovery & Growth Plan (PRGP) savings. Clearly, market 
demand from here could remain highly variable; however, the DB House 
View reflects favorably on EL's end-consumers and addressable market 
prospects, and we believe EL has shown progressive evidence over the past 
12+ months that it is now more firmly in control of its own operations and 
execution. Consequently, we raise our price target to $124 (from $110) on 
the better growth prospects from here, as well as a lower WACC (-50 bps).

Favoring Flavor – Upgrading MKC to Buy
With names like KO, MNST, and EL increasingly appreciated by the market, and 
HPC prospects recently facing more pressure, we are frequently prompted to look 
elsewhere for high-quality value. We believe MKC fits the bill well—benefitting from 
solid trends in its US Consumer business (relatively insulated from challenges 
elsewhere in Food), potentially underappreciated opportunity in International 
Consumer, improving prospects for Flavor Solutions (based on new business wins 
and hopeful volumetric improvement at PEP PFNA and/or within foodservice 
channels), as well as balance sheet optionality for additional targeted bolt-on M&A.
n MKC (Buy, Price Target $75) – While MKC (~20.6x) trades at a notable 
premium to the average Staples company (~19.4x) and certainly to the 
average Food company (~15.6x), we see this valuation premium 3  as 
warranted due to MKC's exposure to the more stable growth spice/
seasoning/extracts/flavors/heat categories, its structural sourcing-driven 
competitive advantages within these categories, and its diversified 
exposure to channels including Food Away from Home. Moreover, 
company management has an excellent operational track record, and we 
see continued opportunistic bolt-on M&A from MKC as positive optionality
—noting a fragmented market both in consumer and flavors outside the US. 
Clearly, there are risks—e.g., developed market demand/elasticity, the 
pace/trajectory of QSR/China recovery, global supply disruption, 
intensifying competition, incremental tariff concerns, consumer trade 
down outstanding, and/or overly aggressive capital allocation. However, 
we believe the company is disciplined in its operational and financial 
approach, and recent tariff relief marginally lowers elasticity/trade down 
risks that we saw as more prominent a few months ago. Overall, we expect 
MKC to deliver stable, volume-biased growth in core US Consumer, with 
improving trends (both top- and bottom-line) in Flavor Solutions and 
overseas (with the company's August acquisition in Mexico an important 
springboard in Latin America). We raise our target price to $75 (from $71) 
on modestly more optimistic top- and bottom-line growth forecasts 
(inclusive of a mark-to-market on FX) and a roll-forward in time.
