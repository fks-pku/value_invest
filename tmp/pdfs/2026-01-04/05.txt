Executive summary
Company snapshot
Ypsomed is headquartered in Burgdorf, Switzerland and is a leading developer and 
manufacturer of drug delivery injection systems for self-medication, in particular 
autoinjectors and pens (60/40). YPSN has divested its lower growth/margin 
Diabetes Care business in August 2025. Instead, the company intends to focus 
solely on its delivery system offering catering to the strongly growing biologics 
market, including GLP-1 drugs, which should result in a step change in its growth 
and margin algorithm over time as divestments are completed.
YPSN should be the #2 in the autoinjector device market with 30-40% share , 
following private peer SHL Medical  that likely holds >50%. YPSN has >130 
customers and claims to work with 27/30 of the largest pharma companies 
worldwide. No customer has >15% share of revenue in FY24/25, which the 
company aims to maintain in the medium term. The company is working on >230 
projects with them, with 70 thereof being commercial compounds. YPSN shared it 
has secured 47 GLP-1 deals with >30 clients, including 5-10 "larger" projects.
The company operates 7 production sites across Switzerland, Germany and China. 
YPSN plans to open a location in the US as well as an additional site in Germany by 
2027. YPSN is in the process of tripling its delivery system production capacity 
from~300m at FY24/25 to ~1bn devices per year by the beginning of the next 
decade, and the group has ~2,000 employees in its continued operations. By 
geography, Ypsomed generated 12% of FY24/25 sales in Switzerland, 60% in 
Europe, 7% in North America, and 21% in the Rest of the World.
The company was founded >40 years ago in 1984 and is still majority owned by the 
founding family with ~72% and run by the founder's son as CEO. In FY24/25, the 
company generated sales from continued operations of CHF 501m and adj. EBIT of 
CHF 167m (a 33.3% margin).
Key points
Step change in growth/margin algorithm later than hoped; fairly valued
YPSN has decided to focus solely on its drug delivery system business going 
forward, which caters to the strongly growing biologics market, including GLP-1 
drugs. As a result, the company divested its lower growth/margin Diabetes Care 
business in August 2025, as well as other smaller businesses, and intends to 
discontinue its contract manufacturing in FY26/27 to free up capacity. We consider 
this a sensible strategic step, which should result in a step change in YPSN's 
growth and margin algorithm over time.
However, new medium-term targets fell short of expectations on core growth in 
the Delivery Systems business (~15% CAGR vs. >20% in recent years), timing, as 
divestments drag down group growth, and margins, which will likely slightly 
decline from currently 33% EBIT for Delivery Systems due to lower pricing of GLP-1 
volumes despite offsetting divestments. With this changed growth and margin 
profile of sales/adj. EBIT/adj. EPS 9%/14%/17% DBe FY25-29 CAGRs, we consider 
the shares fairly valued at 26x P/E 2026, towards lower-end of its 5-year trading 
range (26-40x).

Clear value proposition to customers
Ypsomed offers a range of pre-developed, customizable device platforms 
(autoinjectors, pens) that pharmaceutical companies can license, adapt and brand 
for their own drug products. This presents a straight forward value proposition to 
pharma companies in our view:
n Reduced R&D costs: pharma companies can leverage YPSN's existing, 
proven technology rather than starting from scratch ( YPSN indicates a 
development time of 3-5 years for an entirely new platform).
n Faster time-to-market: using an established device platform can expedite 
regulatory approval and market launch. YPSN indicates 1-2 years from 
project start to being ready for the clinic for customers using its platforms.
n Manufacturing expertise: YPSN has specialized expertise in high-volume, 
high-quality manufacturing of complex medical devices.
n Customization: while based on platforms, devices can be customized in 
terms of design, branding, and specific features to meet the drug's 
requirements.
This is especially true for smaller and medium-sized pharma companies, but this 
can also make sense for very large pharma companies, for example, for a next-
generation delivery system, a biosimiliar, or a regional-specific device - or the 
companies can outsource part of the production for contract manufacturing. In 
general, device development is likely not part of a pharma company's core 
competencies and focus areas.
Ongoing capacity tripling, likely mainly driven by GLP-1s
YPSN is in the process of tripling its delivery system production capacity from 
~300m at FY24/25 to ~1bn devices per year by the beginning of the next decade, 
adding 100-150m in capacity each year. Regionally, the company indicates a 
medium-term capacity split of ~50% in Germany, ~25% in Switzerland, ~15% in the 
US and ~10% in China. The most significant expansion herein is in Germany, which 
should add 400-500m units/year going forward, followed by the setup of the US 
site, and YPSN's first own site in China. YPSN expects about CHF 1.5bn of capex for 
this over the next years, of which ~2/3 should be paid by YPSN and ~1/3 by its 
customers.
We assume that a major part of this expansion is linked to the Novo Nordisk 
CagriSema partnership (expected launch 2027), which should secure significant 
volumes and growth for YPSN. Still, dependence on a single customer should not 
exceed 15% of sales in the medium term. Margins will likely decline slightly from the 
current 33% EBIT for Delivery Systems due to lower pricing of GLP-1 volumes 
despite offsetting divestments. YPSN predominately grows via expansion, and 
therefore this expansion should be the key driver to its medium-term target of 
midpoint of CHF 1bn in sales by FY29/30. The company indicated that in the 
medium term incretins should account for ~1/3 of sales, up from limited sales in 
FY24/25. In general, YPSN expects about 2/3 of its sales to stem from originator 
drugs and 1/3 from biosimilars.
Regarding US tariffs, YPSN should be rather well-protected in the short term as 
only 5-6% of its products are shipped directly to the US (currently 100% from Swiss 
sites) and customers collect products directly from its sites. With the North Carolina 
site opening expected in H1 2028, Ypsomed aims to establish a "local for local" 
presence in the US.

Catering to the injectables market and GLP-1 in an oligopolistic market
YPSN should be the #2 in the autoinjector device market with 30-40% share , 
following private peer SHL Medical that likely holds >50%. This market is positioned 
to benefit from drug innovation in the injectables, as well as increasing biosimilar 
penetration, and the overall trends to more personalized medicine and self-
administration of drugs and should grow at least double-digits. Prominently, 
YPSN's autoinjectors are used for GLP-1 products and the company has secured 47 
GLP-1 deals with >30 clients, including 5-10 "larger" projects. In this business 
quality and reliability of the devices are critical, production scale matters and it 
usually includes long-running production contracts.
YPSN's market definition seems to focus on players with own autoinjector IP, 
wherein SHL Medical and YPSN should remain the two dominant players. Having 
its own IP autoinjector devices should help YPSN in onboarding, in particular, new 
and emerging GLP-1 players over coming years - in contrast to contract 
manufacturers with little or limited own devices.
Despite a number of oral GLP-1 drugs in the pipeline, YPSN expects the oral drug 
penetration to remain at roughly ~20% of the GLP-1 market. Aside from the lower 
efficacy, higher dosage and therefore potentially stronger side effects, YPSN points 
to the fact that significantly higher API requirements (dosage) likely make orals less 
lucrative for pharma companies - at least with this current dosage form.
Financial profile should improve, cash generation likely sufficient
Once YPSN has completed the discontinuation of several businesses, the company 
in its new setup of continued operations (only Delivery Systems) should have a 
meaningfully stronger growth and margin profile. However, given the phasing of 
divestments and dilutive GLP-1 pricing, our estimates point to sales/adj. EBIT/adj. 
EPS CAGRs of 9%/14%/17% FY25-29, implying a 31% margin in FY29/30 (vs. 33.3% 
for Delivery Systems in FY24/25).
Total capex requirements for ongoing capacity to triple over the coming 5 years 
should amount to CHF ~1.5bn. YPSN points to an expected CHF ~300-400m in 
customer prepayments, and combined with a CHF ~300m cash in flow from the 
Diabetes Care sale, strong operating cash flow generation (due to >30% EBIT 
margins) and moderate leverage at 1.7x as of FY24/25, we think YPSN should be 
able to fund these expansion plans. We note that YPSN's expected capex to sales 
ratio of 1.5 to 1.0 for expansions is below that of, for example, CDMO peers (1 to >1).

Valuation & risks
We value Ypsomed based on an average of target 2026/27E P/E and DCF valuations. 
Our target P/E multiple (26x) assumes that 12 months from now, the shares should 
trade at a premium to Ypsomed's closest European-listed peers (Gerresheimer 9x 
2026 P/E, SCHOTT Pharma 17x 2026 P/E), and at a discount to US listed peers, 
based on Bloomberg estimates , (West Pharma 36x 2026E P/E, Stevanato 31x 
2026E P/E), and toward the lower-end of its 5-year trading range (26-40x), which we 
consider fair in light of the company's growth and margin profile.
Key assumptions to our DCF include WACC of 7.8% (based on levered beta of 0.9, 
a risk-free rate of 4%, and an equity risk premium of 5.5% – in line with Deutsche 
Bank's European MedTech & Services coverage) and TG of 2% (in line with long-
term GDP growth). Given the upside potential of ~5% implied by our CHF 345 target 
price, we initiate coverage with a Hold rating.

Company overview
Ypsomed is headquartered in Burgdorf, Switzerland and is a leading developer and 
manufacturer of drug delivery injection systems for self-medication, in particular 
autoinjectors and pens (60/40). The company offers a range of pre-developed, 
customizable device platforms (autoinjectors, pens) that pharmaceutical 
companies can license, adapt and brand for their own drug products. YPSN has 
completed the sale of its lower growth/margin diabetes care segments in August 
2025. Instead, the company intends to focus solely on its delivery system offering, 
catering to the strongly growing biologics market, including GLP-1 drug.
YPSN should be the #2 in the autoinjector device market with 30-40% share, 
following private peer SHL Medical that we estimate holds >50%. YPSN has >130 
customers with no customer having >15% share of revenue in FY24/25, which the 
company aims to maintain in the medium term, and is working on >230 projects 
with them, with 70 thereof being commercial compounds. YPSN claims to work 
with 27 of the 30 largest pharma companies.
The company operates 7 production sites across Switzerland, Germany and China. 
YPSN plans to open a location in the US as well as an additional site in Germany by 
2027. In the FY24/25, the company added capacity of 120m units/year and reached 
a disposable capacity of ~300m units/year and the group had ~2,000 employees in 
its continued operations. By geography, YPSN generated 12% of FY24/25 sales in 
Switzerland, 60% in Europe, 7% in North America, and 21% in the Rest of the World.
The company was founded >40 years ago in 1984 and is still majority owned by the 
founding family with ~72%. In FY24/25, the company generated sales from 
continued operations of CHF 501m and adj. EBIT of CHF 167m (a 33.3% margin).
Delivery Systems
The Delivery Systems division provides pharma and biotech customers with 
autoinjectors and pens designed for administering liquid medications. The 
segment will be YPSN's sole focus going forward. It is important to note that YPSN 
produces merely the plastic casing of the autoinjector or pen, not the containment 
solution (e.g. cartridge, syringe) within the device. The figure below shows 
autoinjectors on the left, pens on the right, the patch injector YpsoDose in the

Others and divested business segments
In FY24/25, the company's annual report still comprised the divested Diabetes Care 
business as well as other businesses:
n Diabetes Care business (27% of FY24/25 total sales) : a B2C business 
(brand: mylife) for diabetes customers. Its portfolio includes insulin pumps, 
infusions sets, pen needles, blood glucose monitoring systems and other 
diabetes supplies. The products are distributed via subsidiaries to 
hospitals, doctors, pharmacies and patients, and via distributors (pen 
needles). The business did CHF 237m of revenue and likely about a CHF 
~34m EBIT loss in FY24/25. YPSN has completed the sale of the business 
for CHF 420m to TecMed AG in August 2025, also owned by majority YPSN 
owner Willy Michel.
n Other businesses (3% of FY24/25 total sales) : Includes Ypsotec and 
properties not used for operational purposes. Ypsotec is a subsidiary in 
Czechia and a supplier of precision-turned parts and components, which 
was sold in October 2025.
Customer and therapeutic area concentration
YPSN states it currently has >130 customers (with ~30 in incretins) and >230 
clinical and commercial programs with them, split into 160 clinical and 70 
commercial. As of FY24/25, no single customer contributed >15% of sales, which 
the company aims to maintain in the medium term, and the Top 6 seem to account 
for ~50%.
By therapeutic area, Diabetes and Obesity account for ~10% as of FY24/25, but 
should increase meaningfully to ~1/3 of group revenue tied to incretins, i.e. GLP-1s. 
Autoimmune currently accounts for the largest portion of ~35%, followed by 
Diabetes, Insulin at ~25% and Hormone Therapies at ~20%. These are followed by 
smaller revenue contributors namely Neurology (~5%), Cardiovascular (~5%), Rare 
Diseases (~2%), Oncology (~2%) and Infectious Diseases (~2%).
For GLP-1s, YPSN points to a balanced portfolio between Originators and

Growth drivers for YPSN's operations
YPSN's business is positioned to benefit from the following growth drivers:
n YPSN's products should help simplify self-care by providing innovative, 
user-friendly injection systems for self-administration and digital solutions 
that empower patients with chronic conditions to manage their own 
medication safely and effectively.
n Biologics are drugs produced in living cells using complex 
biotechnological processes. They play a significant role in treating many 
chronic diseases, differing from traditional chemical drugs due to their size 
and complexity, and are often administered intravenously in liquid form or 
very often subcutaneously, leading to an increase in demand in Ypsomed´s 
injectors. Biologics make up a significant proportion of the pipeline of new 
drugs.
n Biosimilars are cost-effective, highly similar versions of approved biologic 
drugs that become available after patent expiration, offering no significant 
clinical differences from their original counterparts. This should help 
expand patient access to vital injectable therapies for chronic conditions. 
Combined with increased affordability and a growing number of biologics 
losing patent protection, this should drive higher demand for delivery 
system for companies like Ypsomed.
n GLP-1 drugs are used to treat diabetes and obesity, currently 
predominately via subcutaneous self-administration by patients via 
autoinjectors or pens. The GLP-1 market is expected to experience strong 
growth, likely resulting in higher delivery system volumes for YPSN.
n The Hospital-to-home trend, fueled by healthcare budget pressures and 
rising patient volumes, should likely increase demand for YPSN's self-
injection systems and digital health offerings by enabling patients to self-
administer medications and monitor conditions at home.
Strategic shift
Over the past years, YPSN has taken steps to focus exclusively on its higher growth/
margin Delivery Systems business going forward. The most material step was the 
divestment of its once core business Diabetes Care in August 2025, which had 
underperformed for a number of years, in our view, reporting declining sales and 
operating losses. Conversely, the Delivery Systems saw accelerating growth and 
likely profitability, explaining the eventual strategic shift. The Diabetes Care  
business was sold to the company TecMed, founded by Ypsomed's founder, Willy

YPSN also sold its smaller Ypsotec business during 2025 and intends to discontinue

sensible strategic steps that should enable the company to enhance both its growth 
and margin algorithm over time.

Company history
Ypsomed's history began in 1984 with the founding of Disetronic by brothers Willy 
and Peter Michel. Disetronic had been founded to develop, manufacture and sell 
infusion pumps, in particular a small insulin pump, and expanded into the injection 
systems business in 1986. In 2003, Willy Michel sold the infusion business to 
Roche, but retained the injection business, which then became Ypsomed. The 
name is derived from "Ipso" (self) and "med" (medication) to reflect its core focus 
on self-medication devices. Ypsomed went public on the Swiss Stock Exchange in 
2003.
Since its inception, Ypsomed has focused on innovative self-care solutions, 
investing heavily in research and development. The company adopted a platform 
model for pen development around 2006, significantly reducing development 
times. Key milestones include the strategic acquisition of the German company 
"Diabetes Care" in 2011. Diabetes Care made up the majority of the business and 
the growth still in FY17, followed then by declines and an acceleration of the 
Delivery Systems segment from FY18 onwards. Following a period of 
underperformance, Ypsomed has sold the Diabetes Care business in August 2025 
to exclusively focus on the growing Delivery Systems business, marking a major 
strategic step in the company's development. In addition, the company has 
divested its smaller Ypsotec business and discontinued contract manufacturing.

Market overview
Market snapshot
YPSN should be the #2 in the autoinjector device market with 30-40% share, 
following private peer SHL Medical that likely holds >50%. This market is positioned 
to benefit from drug innovation in the injectables, as well as increasing biosimilar 
penetration, and the overall trends to more personalized medicine and self-
administration of drugs and should grow at least double digits. Prominently, 
YPSN's autoinjectors are used for GLP-1 products. In this business, quality and 
reliability of the devices are critical, production scale matters and it usually includes 
long-running production contracts. This market should experience strong >10% 
growth fueled by new injectable drug approvals and be further boosted by the high 
GLP-1 volumes expected going forward. For the incretin (GLP-1 & others) market, 
YPSN points to an  18% CAGR from 2024-30E.
YPSN's market definition seems to focus on players with own autoinjector IP, where 
SHL Medical and YPSN should remain the two dominant players. Having its own IP 
autoinjector devices should help YPSN in onboarding, in particular, new and 
emerging GLP-1 players over coming years - in contrast to contract manufacturers 
with no or limited own devices.
YPSN itself does not provide a market size estimate, however, for example, peer 
Stevanato has pointed to a $2.1bn addressable market for drug delivery systems, 
expected to grow >10% going forward, which likely has slightly broader definition.
An oligopolistic market structure with comparably high entry barriers
The market for drug delivery devices has an oligopolistic market structure and the 
largest players are YPSN and SHL Medical, mainly producing their own IP devices, 
and Gerresheimer and West Pharma, mainly active in contract manufacturing. This 
market should experience strong >10% growth fueled by new injectable drug 
approvals and further boosted by the high GLP-1 volumes expected going forward.
Below we describe the market characteristics:
n In-sourcing risk of the drug delivery device for small- and medium-sized 
pharma customers is likely limited given cost and development time/risk. 
However, for the largest players with significant volumes, this could be 
sensible.
n Contracts should be rather of strategic nature, likely spanning ~10 years.

Competitive risk from oral GLP-1 candidates
Oral and injectable incretin therapies both have substantial benefits and limitations. 
Oral incretin therapies  offer advantages in terms of convenience, storage and 
transport. However, they face challenges such as daily dosing, lower tolerability 
and reduced bioavailability. Injectable incretin therapies , on the other hand, 
provide strong efficacy and ease of use with less frequent dosing, ensuring they 
remain a key part of incretin therapy despite minor drawbacks like needle phobia 
and supply chain complexity.
Despite a number of oral GLP-1 drugs in the pipeline, YPSN expects the oral drug 
penetration to remain at roughly ~20% of the GLP-1 market it has now. Aside from 
the lower efficacy, higher dosage and therefore potentially stronger side effects, 
YPSN points to the fact that significantly higher API requirements (dosage) likely 
make orals less lucrative for pharma companies - at least with this current dosage 
form.

The rise of biologics requires more drug injectable solutions
Small molecules have been dominating the drug manufacturing market for a long 
time, as more than 80% of current drugs are based on small molecules. These tend 
to be more established, less complex, and less capital-intensive to produce. 
However, large molecules (i.e. biologics) are quickly rising in popularity and 
importance, as they are revolutionizing therapeutic treatment in many major 
therapeutic areas through ground-breaking progress in molecular biology and 
genetics.
The production of biopharmaceuticals is a complex and lengthy process that 
involves several different steps. It is a challenging manufacturing process because 
biopharmaceuticals are based on large molecules that are produced by living 
organisms (bacteria, mammalian cells, etc.) as opposed to chemically synthesised 
drugs. These chemical medications are based on small molecules and can therefore 
be absorbed by the gastrointestinal tract (ingested as pills or capsules), whereas 
biopharmaceuticals are injected or infused. This is the only way that they can be 

=== PAGE 32 ===

3 December 2025

Ypsomed
Page 32 Deutsche Bank AG
absorbed by the human body. Post injection, biopharmaceuticals bind to only 
specific cell receptors and therefore have a more targeted effect than chemical 
drugs. Given this effect, they open up new opportunities to treat patients suffering 
from severe or rare diseases.
The number of approved biologics has increased significantly in recent years, which 
is expected to further accelerate going forward. According to Evaluate Pharma, 
they are expected to account for ~50% of newly-approved drugs by 2025. In the 
year 2022, biologics made up to 40% of newly approved drugs. This should be the 
main driver behind the expected increased capacity requirements in the future.

Historical performance
YPSN's historical performance paints a volatile picture, with group sales growing 
up to FY17, followed by sales declines and a re-acceleration from FY20 onwards - 
with similar development for earnings. This is explained by divergent performance 
of the company's two segments, as Diabetes Care made up the majority of the 
business and the growth still in FY17, followed then by declines and an acceleration 
of the Delivery Systems segment from FY18 onwards. In numbers, the group 
delivered a 5% sales growth CAGR from FY14-20, split into a 14% CAGR for Delivery 
Systems and -1% for Diabetes Care. Over FY20-23 group growth accelerated to an 
11% CAGR, mainly driven by a 22% CAGR for Delivery Systems, while Diabetes 
Care continued to decline (-5% CAGR). This period likely saw accelerating demand 
for drug delivery devices and comparably softer demand for diabetes care 
products, couple with resulting divestments in Diabetes Care.
Profitability (only available for the group) was around a 13-16% EBIT margin 
between FY15-18 followed by a sharp drop to <3% in FY19 and FY20 driven by a 
combination of issues in the Diabetes Care segment including weaker growth in 
insulin pumps, a changed FDA approval strategy as well as termination of a larger 
manufacturing contract. Over FY21-23 profitability improved as growth in the likely 
more profitable Delivery Systems segment accelerated. We think the Diabetes Care 
business fell short of growth and margin expectations over the past couple of years, 
which likely ultimately resulted in the recent decision to divest this former core 
business entirely.

Peer group analysis
Asmall range of listed direct competitors
Ypsomed competes with numerous different companies in the broader drug 
containment and drug delivery solutions markets. These can be either privately 
owned or publicly listed, and range from small local players, through midsize 
companies, to large global enterprises and conglomerates with sizeable medical 
delivery solutions divisions.
However, when considering Ypsomed's focus areas of injection and infusion 
systems for self-medication, the company operates in relatively concentrated 
markets with high barriers to entry (regulatory and quality requirements, required 
know-how, supply chain integrity, investments in manufacturing and R&D). As a 
global leader in drug delivery solutions, Ypsomed competes with a few dominant 
players, which together control the majority of the underlying market. Although 
Ypsomed is the only listed pure-play drug delivery injection systems for self-
medication, we have identified Stevanato Group, Gerresheimer, SCHOTT Pharma 
and West Pharmaceutical Services as the most relevant listed peers.
One can also compare Ypsomed to other companies that offer drug containment 
and drug delivery solutions, such as Becton Dickinson, Daetwyler and AptarGroup.

Leading market position and differentiated offering
In contrast to competitors (such as Stevanato or Gerresheimer), Ypsomed only 
offers products that are related to drug delivery injection systems for self-
medication. It does not offer drug containment solutions, moulded glass or 
equipment. This focused offering creates a unique investment case that is solely 
focused on the self administration / home care trend. The company should have 
limited exposure to Pharma drug pipeline risks, as it works with 27 out of the 30 
largest pharma companies and no customer contributes >15% of sales, which 
should be maintained over the mid term despite the large Novo Nordisk CagriSema 
contract. We note that the Top 6 customers account for ~50% of group sales in 
FY24/25.
Certain limitations apply when comparing peers
With regard to the most relevant publicly listed competitors, we note the following 
limitations:
Stevanato provides integrated solutions for drug containment, drug delivery, and 
diagnostics to the pharmaceutical, biotech, and life sciences sectors. They 
specialize in glass primary packaging (vials, syringes, cartridges, ampoules) in bulk 
and ready-to-fill formats. Additionally, they offer machinery for glass conversion, 
inspection systems, and assembly/packaging solutions, alongside high-quality 
plastic solutions for medical and diagnostic use.
The company derives ~85% of group revenue from its Biopharmaceutical and 
Diagnostic Solutions segment, where the company offers a broad range of Drug 
Containment Solutions, Drug Delivery Systems (i.e., medical devices) and In-Vitro-
Diagnostics (IVD) Solutions. However, <10% of group revenue should be directly 
comparable to Ypsomed's offerings as Ypsomed only provides products that are 
related to drug delivery injection systems for self-medication (it does not offer Drug 
Containment or IVD Solutions like Stevanato does).
In terms of operating performance, we note that Stevanato has seen meaningfully 
lower EBITDA margins than Ypsomed, despite being the larger company (in terms 
of revenue). This should be attributable to Ypsomed's focus on high margin 
products, in our view. Stevanato's sales/earnings growth prospects appear to be 
lower when looking at YPSN's medium-term targets of its continued operations 
(midpoint 15% revenue CAGR), although we acknowledge the ongoing 
divestments make Bloomberg estimates appear lower over 2024-26.
In addition, the following points stand out when comparing the two companies:
n Stevanato uses earnings adjustments (e.g., ramp-up costs of newly 
constructed facilities), while Ypsomed does not.
n Stevanato has a significantly higher addressable market, given its broad 
product portfolio.
n Stevanato has >700 customers, while Ypsomed has >130 customers.
n Stevanato is spending large capex amounts on major expansion projects 
(23% of sales net capex guided for FY25), however, still less in total and

higher capital intensity.

Risks
Rate of biological drug approvals
Ypsomed provides containment solutions for injectable drugs. Therefore, its 
growth going forward is linked to the rate of biological drug approvals, as these are 
typically injectable drugs. If the approval rate were to decelerate/accelerate, this 
would negatively/positively affect the company.
Oral Adoption
Ypsomed's strategic focus is on autoinjectors and pens for injectable drugs. While 
the markets for oral and injectable drugs are often viewed as distinct, each with its 
own advantages and disadvantages, there's a possibility that oral incretins could 
increasingly replace their injectable counterparts. Conversely, the market for 
injectable drugs could also experience expansion due to their superior drug efficacy 
and higher API requirements which make the drug production more costly for 
pharmaceutical companies, thereby ensuring continued demand.
Competitive industry
Ypsomed operates in a highly competitive industry. The company has leading 
positions within its industry, and it continues to create, invest in, or acquire 
advanced technology, which increases its ability to maintain or build on those 
leading positions. However, any delay in the development, introduction, or 
marketing of new products or new versions of existing products could impact the 
company's market share and financial position. On the flip side, faster development 
and introduction of products could also positively affect YPSN's operations. 
Similarly, the company could be challenged by the emergence of new technologies, 
but potentially also invent these new technologies itself and benefit from them.
Customer Concentration
Currently, YPSN points to a diversified customer portfolio with no single client 
accounting for more than 15% of commercial revenue. However, going forward 
customer concentration is likely to increase given, in particular, the large GLP-1 
contract YPSN has with Novo Nordisk. This should increase YPSN's dependence on 
the success of individual customers and their compounds, providing an upside/
downside risk. Such partnerships with increasing volumes and customer 
concentration, however, can also help ensure significant volumes in the mid-term 
and thereby support growth - if successful.
Therapeutic Area Concentration
Ypsomed faces a risk of therapeutic area concentration due to its strong focus on 
injectable drugs, especially for GLP-1s. Therefore, the company's growth is partly 
linked to the success/failure of these injectable drugs, or stronger/weaker-than-
anticipated demand. This could lead to vulnerability if demand shifts or if new, non-
injectable GLP-1 delivery methods emerge. However, strong growth is predicted for 
the incretin market and also expansion into new medical areas, which could enable 
YPSN to position itself as a key player in this segment.
Innovation
Ypsomed's ability to continue to maintain and grow its business depends on its 
ability to identify and successfully develop new innovative products. If the company 
is unable to maintain a cost-effective flow of successful new products that are 
sufficient to maintain and grow its business, cover any sales erosion due to

Available capacity and capacity constraints
A risk of Ypsomed's refined strategic focus, particularly its exclusive concentration 
on pens and autoinjectors following the divestment of its diabetes segment, is the 
potential for overcapacity. Should the company become overly reliant on the GLP-1 
market without a sufficiently broad customer base, or if customer requirements 
shift unexpectedly, a singular focus could lead to substantial excess production

capabilities also could position Ypsomed to benefit more strongly from the GLP-1 
delivery device market if demand continues or exceeds its projected strong growth 
trajectory. Ypsomed could also benefit if competitors would be struggling with 
meeting market needs.
Healthcare reforms
Ypsomed operates in a sector that is heavily affected by regulatory framework. 
Future healthcare reforms or tightening could create headwinds. Reforms might 
weigh on reimbursement for diagnostic products or solutions. The resulting margin 
pressure on Ypsomed's customers might be passed on through the value chain, 
which could make the company's offering less attractive for its customers. On the 
other hand, this might also positively influence Ypsomed's business model.
Global economic downturn
While we consider Ypsomed's business to be largely independent of the economic 
cycle, we cannot rule out temporary spillover effects and a compression of sector 
multiples in a broad economic downturn. Ypsomed could also potentially sell/place 
fewer instruments in an economic downturn, given that utilization of the existing 
systems tends to be below 100%.
Product recalls
The company is subject to a number of laws and regulations that require it to report 
adverse events associated with its products. Such adverse events and potential 
health risks may lead to voluntary or mandatory market actions, including product 
recalls, product withdrawals and field correction actions. These actions could result 
in significant costs and potential disruptions in the supply of its products. On the 
other hand, Ypsomed might not be impacted by product recalls.
Global supply chain
Ypsomed is engaged in the manufacturing and sourcing of products and materials 
on a global scale. The company's operations and those of its suppliers could be 
disrupted by a number of local issues, including: disruptions in logistics, strikes, 
loss of key suppliers, supplier capacity constraints, quality/safety issues, epidemics 
and pandemics, and natural and man-made disasters. In addition, the company 
relies on limited sources of supply for many components in its production, and any 
disruption from one of these suppliers could impact the company's business.
Potential gains/losses due to litigation charges
The company is subject to risks arising from legal disputes involving either the 
company itself or any third party that it deals with. Any impact on the company may 
result in financial gains/losses on account of punitive damage payments, equitable 
remedies, fines, and criminal/civil sanctions.
