Deal Or No Deal? Organic & Inorganic 
Paths To Value, Initiate At BUY, PT $28
What's in this note?
We initiate coverage on First Horizon (ticker FHN; market cap of $11b and total 
assets of $83b), with a Buy rating and TP of $28; representing 18% upside potential 
vs. the 12/15 closing price. In this note, we discuss our thesis on FHN and run 
through our model assumptions.
Investment thesis
n FHN has had a "noisy" few years post the aborted tie-up with TD Bank, 
which was withdrawn in 2023 and had left FHN in limbo after its 2020 
merger of equals with IBERIABANK. Management quickly pivoted to 
focusing on being independent and making a number of investments back 
into the business that were postponed due to the TD deal. Management is 
now re-focusing on organic growth initiatives that should improve returns 
and profitability. We see the opportunity for loan growth re-acceleration, 
strong expense control, profitability improvement and a balanced rate 
positioning. This positive transition as well as the possibility for 
participating in an M&A deal in what should be a mostly positive operating 
backdrop in 2026 should lead to a further re-rating of shares. Buy.
We are favorable on FHN shares and rate as Buy due to the following:
n Loan growth re-acceleration. Management expects to grow loans by mid-
single digits in FY26 after minimal balance-sheet growth in the wake of the 
withdrawn TD Bank  transaction. This will likely put FHN in-line with 
expectations for most of its midcap-bank peers after lagging over the past 
few years. There are a number of initiatives to deepen relationships with 
current clients that are expected to help grow loans, such as new product 
offerings and production from new hires. Additionally, as rates are 
expected to continue to decline, this should lead to growth in loans to 
mortgage companies (FHN's highest-yielding portfolio), stabilization/some 
growth in CRE (which has been a headwind due to higher rates) and growth 
in C&I.
n Strong expense control. Management has been making investments in its 
technology stack the years since the withdrawal of the TD Bank deal and 
expects to achieve efficiencies in its operations while harvesting the 
benefits of investments made. Management expects flattish expenses in 
2026 excluding incremental commissions (from the counter-cyclical 
businesses) while continuing to make investments to drive future revenue

Bank description
n First Horizon runs 412 branches across 12 states, primarily across the 
southeast US with headquarters in Memphis, Tennessee. FHN has $83b of 
assets (and $65b of deposits) and is ranked #33 by assets as of 9/30. FHN 
has grown through acquisitions recently with acquisitions of Capital Bank 
(~$10b of assets in 2017), entered into an MOE with IBERIABANK (~$32b 
of assets in 2020) and acquired 30 branches from TFC post their MOE (~$2b 
of assets in 2020). FHN did attempt a merger with TD Bank beginning in Feb 
2022 but TD Bank terminated its deal with FHN because it could not receive 
regulatory approval given AML issues. FHN delayed certain investments 
due to the tie up with TD, but has resumed these activities and put most of 
the delayed investments behind it (although investment spend continues).
n Business segments. FHN operates three operating segments: 1) 
Commercial, Consumer, and Wealth, 2) Wholesale, and 3) 
Corporate.
n Revenue mix. 75-80% net interest income, 20-25% fee income.
n Loan mix. 55% C&I, 22% CRE, and 25% Consumer.
n Deposits. 35% Promotional/CDs, 24% non-interest bearing, 21% 
Base Rate, 17% Indexed, and 3% Brokered deposits.

n Geographic footprint. We believe FHN has strong franchise value given a

n Countercyclical businesses. With rates expected to be cut a few more 
times by the Fed, the combination of the fixed-income business, loans to 
mortgage companies and mortgage helps provide a hedge to offset 
pressure in net interest income. If there are fewer-than-expected rate cuts, 
then net interest income should be higher. These two help offset each other 
and provide stability in earnings that could lead to less downward earnings 
revisions vs. peers in a down market.

n 15%+ adjusted ROTCE target. FHN hit the 15% mark in 3Q25, but that 
included some lumpy one-timers and is not viewed as sustainable. 
Management believes it can obtain a sustainable 15%+ ROTCE over the 
next few years that be driven by a number of levers. These include 1) revenue

Negatives
n Current loan growth. YTD loan growth has been muted, with period-end 
loan growth of about 1% vs. nearly 4% for the banking industry. 
Management is optimistic that loans can grow MSD in FY26, with lower 
rates acting as a catalyst to boost loan growth as well as a number of 
organic initiatives resulting from the delayed residual benefits of prior deals 
and benefits from current investments.
n Deposit composition. Higher cost promotional deposits & CDs contribute 
35% of the deposit base vs. 31% in 1Q24. Deposit competition is fierce in 
the southeast region but retention levels have been impressive over the past 
several qtrs, remaining in the mid- to high-90s. Short durations allow 
management to price down these deposits with further rate cuts expected, 
but costs could linger higher if the Fed pauses its cutting cycle sooner than 
expected.
n M&A expectations/valuation re-rating risks.  On the 3Q earnings call, 
management implied it could be a buyer of other businesses, counter to 
market expectations that it could be a seller. On the call, the CEO noted that 
the near-term focus is unchanged with priorities on organic growth

the deposits from there. FHN has done a good job retaining a significant 
portion of these balances.

Growth through M&A
n FHN has participated in a number of M&A transactions throughout its 
history. Since 2017, FHN acquired Capital Bank (~$10b of assets in 2017), 
entered into an MOE with IBERIABANK (~$32b of assets in 2020) and 
acquired 30 branches from TFC post their MOE (~$2b of assets in 2020). TD 
Bank made a ~40% cash offer in February 2022. However, in May 2023, TD 
Bank terminated its deal with FHN (a few days after First Republic failed) 
because it could not receive regulatory approval given AML issues.
TD Bank proposed merger
n At the time of the deal proposal on Feb 28, 2022, the pro-forma bank would 
have been the sixth largest US bank. However the deal was terminated due 
to TD not getting the regulatory approvals needed given AML issues. This 
was all on TD, not on FHN.
n TD described FHN as an attractive target based on:
n FHN's "attractive markets in the Southeastern U.S."
n 1+ million customer base in fast-growing markets.
n Long history, strong leadership and attractive returns.
n Market-leading share in Tennessee and large footprint in Louisiana.
n FHN's "growth mindset and disciplined risk culture"
n Delayed integration. Post the MOE with IBERIABANK, FHN put on hold the 
conversion of the primary client treasury management system due to the 
announced TD merger. However, TD did not receive the regulatory 
approvals to proceed with the deal given AML issues. FHN completed the 
treasury management conversion in 2024.
First Horizon and Iberia Bank MOE
n The no-premium MOE transaction allowed FHN to grow from ~$49b in total 
assets to $83b at the time of the July 2020 deal closing. The deal added scale 
and the ability to invest in technology and to expand product offerings.
