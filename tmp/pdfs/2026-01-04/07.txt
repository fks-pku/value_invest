Updated Valuation Methodology; PT to $20
We increase our PT to $20 from $4.50 as we increase estimates, update our 
valuation methodology, and roll valuation forward one year to CY2027 (PL’s FY28). 
The key difference in our valuation approach is the introduction of Software and 
Services peers due to the company’s shift in providing AI-enabled solutions for 
customers as well as factoring in our higher revenue expectations for PL. We now 
value PL at a ~12x 2027 EV/sales multiple on 2027 sales of $477mn. Our ~12x 
multiple is based on relative weightings ascribed to the multiple implied by a 
correlation analysis between EV/sales and expected revenue growth for Software 
and Services peers and the median ‘new Space’ multiple. We had previously 
leveraged a ~3.5x EV multiple on CY26 revenue. We note PL is currently trading at 
~11x 2027 EV/Sales.

PL's Evolving Business Model 
A Business Model Shift 
PL, while still building and operating satellites, has shifted from selling raw imagery and 
data subscriptions to offering dedicated satellite capacity, complemented by layering AI-
enabled analytics on top of its data. While this involves increased capital investments, 
fleet build costs are largely funded by upfront customer payments, which helps to 
mitigate cash funding risk and limits potential balance-sheet strain. Against this backdrop, 
while we are enthused by PL’s recent momentum and strategic shift toward satellite 
services and AI-enabled analytics, we note that satellite services is relatively new territory 
for PL, with the first large multi-year constellation deal signed in January 2025 with JSAT. 
Record Backlog; Potential for Further Upside
Notably, PL’s backlog has tripled to ~$734mn as of 3QFY26, largely driven by new 
satellite services awards. At its Investor Day in October 2025, management highlighted 
that it is currently qualifying 20 opportunities in its pipeline with an average total contract 
value of ~$170mn, largely representing sizeable, long-duration contracts. On its 3QFY26 
earnings call, management reiterated that while the broader pipeline remains robust, only 
~6 of these opportunities are currently at a more mature stage, with timing still subject to 
typical procurement uncertainties. If these materialize, we could see incremental upside to 
PL’s record backlog and enhanced long-term revenue visibility, though we are mindful of 
award timing, near-term mix pressure during build phases (dependent on contract type), 
and potential execution risk on initial satellite services programs. 
Balancing Growth and Margin Volatility
We recognize this business model presents somewhat of a departure from PL’s 
historically software and data led subscription model, introducing near-term mix-driven 
margin volatility even as the addressable market and customer set expand. However, 
customer-funded satellite fleet builds help moderate cash burn and balance sheet risk. 
Given these dynamics, we remain measured in incorporating incremental upside to our 
financial estimates given early adoption characteristics and near-term mix-driven margin 
volatility (e.g., hardware vs. services). However, the expanded backlog offers visibility into 
future growth which could provide upside to multiples for PL, in our view. Additionally, 
the company’s investments in providing AI-enabled solutions for end-customers introduce 
a higher multiple peer set than traditional Space peers. 
New Satellite Services Model Offerings 
Planet’s new satellite services span across three offerings including dedicated capacity, 
direct access, and constellation ownership. 
• Dedicated capacity provides customers with a guaranteed portion of Planet’s 
existing constellations without requiring hardware ownership. Under the dedicated 
capacity model, PL manages operations while still securing access and priority 
tasking for the customer. 
• Direct access enables direct and secure taking and data delivery through the 
customer’s own infrastructure. While Planet retains ownership and operations of 
the constellation, the customer controls when and where imaging occurs.

Owl: PL’s Next-Generation Monitoring Fleet
Owl represents PL’s next-generation monitoring fleet, designed to deliver near-daily, ~1-
meter-class imagery with lower latency and enhanced processing capabilities on the 
satellite itself. Unlike the current SuperDove fleet, Owl incorporates NVIDIA (covered by 
Joe Moore) graphics processing units (GPUs) to enable AI processing at the edge, allowing 
analytics such as change detection, activity monitoring, and rapid alerting to occur directly 
on the satellite before the data is sent back to Earth. 
Management views Owl as a key enabler of expanded security, disaster-response, and 
rapid-insight applications, and a strategic step toward more automated, insight-driven 
geospatial intelligence. The first technology demonstration satellite is targeted for launch 
in late 2026, with Owl expected to complement and gradually upgrade Planet’s existing 
broad-area monitoring constellation over time.
The Growth of Imaging Capabilities in Defense Scenarios 
In our view, government customers are increasingly viewing imaging capabilities as a 
mission-critical asset rather than a supplementary enhancement in the context of defense. 
Sovereign imaging capabilities and Space access, persistent Earth observation, rapid 
tasking, and an elevated global threat environment underpin the need for early threat 
detection, maritime domain awareness, and border security, delivering decision making 
advantages. Demand for sovereign access is increasing, pushing governments towards 
dedicated capacity and direct access constructs. 
The addition of AI-driven analytics turns imagery into actionable alerts, shortening 
decision making times, and improving productivity. While we acknowledge that additional 
contracts must clear cost and mission-fit hurdles, we see increasing global defense 
budgets providing a tailwind to the procurement of multi-year commitments, making 
imaging a core element of defense capabilities rather than a discretionary supplement. 
Commercial Momentum and Emerging Use Cases
PL’s commercial business represents a significant long-term opportunity despite near-term 
softness (revenue down ~8% YoY) tied to agricultural seasonality and a strategic focus on 
larger customers. A key example of PL’s expanding enterprise relevance is its new contract 
with AXA, one of the world’s largest global insurance groups. AXA will integrate PL’s 
monitoring data, and high-resolution tasking into its digital claims platform to improve 
loss assessment and disaster-response workflows, and a marketplace component will 
make Planet’s products available to AXA’s broader network of insurance partners. 
While commercial adoption remains gradual, these engagements illustrate how Planet’s 
solutions can expand into insurance, finance and other data-driven industries over time.

Structural Tailwinds Supporting a Broader Space Opportunity
The opportunity set for commercial Space players is broad and of long-duration, spanning 
across launch services, satellite manufacturing and communications, Earth observation 
and data analytics, and the increasing need for space-based defense capabilities. Structural 
tailwinds are reshaping the Space sector and commercial participation is increasing on 
lower capital needs and scalable technologies. At the same time, launch costs are falling 
and global defense and space budgets are growing, expanding the TAM across government 
and commercial customers, unlocking incremental demand as launch cadence rises and 
economics improve. 
Profitability Emerging as a Key Differentiator
Barriers to entry are easing as the economics, technology, and infrastructure of Space 
continue to mature. The increasing availability of commercial launch services, increased 
investments from private markets, and public-private partnerships have made it easier for 
new entrants to deploy, operate, and monetize Space-based assets. Many Space 
companies, however, remain unprofitable, given high capital intensity, lumpy milestone-
based revenue, and relatively nascent end markets, along with an elevated cost of capital. 
Against this backdrop, the market, in our view, is increasingly rewarding Space names that 
prove the ability to scale while also achieving profitability and FCF generation.

Revenue Surprises Dictate Post-Print Performance
PL’s stock tends to exhibit outsized moves following earnings reports, with the market 
reaction largely driven by revenue surprises. Since 2024, PL’s stock has consistently 
underperformed when topline results missed consensus or guidance came in below 
expectations, often triggering volatility. 
In 2QFY25, When PL missed revenue estimates by ~1% and provided forward guidance 
below estimates, the stock ended the day down ~28%. In contrast, quarters featuring 
revenue beats paired with constructive guidance or proof of profitability have typically led 
to a rally in the stock. PL’s revenue beats have been supported by large new contracts, 
specifically in Defense & Intelligence and Civil Government. After reporting 3QFY26 
earnings and beating revenue estimates by ~13% (revenue of ~$81.3mn vs. consensus of ~
$72mn), PL's stock ended the day up ~35%. 
Beats have also coincided with faster activation of sizeable bookings, contract renewals 
and expansions with strategic customers, and greater adoption of analytics/AI-enabled 
offerings. This reflects management’s focus on expanding multi-year agreements and 
growing its base of recurring, contract-driven revenue, alongside an ongoing shift toward 
higher-value data and insight-driven solutions. Conversely, revenue misses have reflected 
timing delays on deals or delayed customer starts. 
Shifted Focus to Profitability
Beyond the topline, forward guidance and profitability signals have also been key in stock 
price movement after earnings reports. FY26, in our view, marked a shift in investor focus 
from pure growth to growth alongside profitability. In 4QFY25, PL achieved its first 
quarter of positive adj. EBITDA of ~$2.4mn as a strong topline and disciplined cost 
management proved effective. In 1QFY26, PL reported its first quarter of positive FCF 
($8mn) and in 2QFY26, delivered its second quarter of positive FCF (~$46mn) while also 
noting that it expects FY26 to be FCF positive – a year ahead of schedule. After reporting 
1QFY26 and 2QFY26 earnings, the stock was up ~49% and ~48%, respectively. Positive 
FCF was driven by positive adj. EBITDA as well as working capital tailwinds, driven by cash 
payments tied to customer-funded contracts. These milestones led to sharp rallies in the 
stock as investors gained confidence in the company’s profitability trajectory and PL’s 
ability to sustain cash generation while achieving operating scale.
Consecutive Quarters of Positive Adj. EBITDA and FCF Generation

Evolving Contract Structures 
That said, PL is entering a new paradigm as its recently awarded contracts introduce 
hardware and service-heavy structures. Importantly, the satellite fleet build is generally 
customer-funded through upfront cash payments, which helps moderate cash burn and 
working capital risk during initial build phases. While these broaden PL’s TAM and improve

recent momentum, the business model shift needs to be proven out. As PL executes on 
these new contracts, we are looking for sustained execution across PL’s evolving satellite 
services framework with continued progress on margin expansion, cash conversion, and 
profitability.

Comping PL to Software and ‘New Space’ Peers
PL’s broader comp group, in our view, includes a diverse set of players across the 
Software and Data Analytics markets, which we see as accounting for subscription/usage-
based business model economics. We also include government services peers in this comp 
set given PL’s increasing shift towards government markets (D&I and Civil Government 
made up ~84% of revenue in 3QFY26). This peer group suggests PL should trade at ~15x 
EV/sales given our forecasted ~26% growth CAGR for the business. 
Spire (SPIR) and BlackSky (BKSY), which are remote-sensing companies with notable 
government revenue bases, are arguably PL’s closest public comps, trade at ~1.7x and 
~3.9x 2027 EV/sales respectively. However, we note differences in their respective 
business models. PL operates the largest daily-imaging constellation and is expanding into 
satellite services with AI-analytics. BlackSky focuses on high-resolution satellite images 
and real-time geospatial intelligence, focusing on frequent revisits to areas. Meanwhile, 
Spire focuses on radio frequency data, operating a fleet of small satellites that collect 
data on weather, maritime, and aviation activity. We note that BKSY’s and SPIR’s revenue 
growth, based on consensus estimates, is ~14% and ~33% vs. current consensus estimates 
of ~25% for PL (25E-27E CAGR). 
We arrive at PL’s 2027 EV/Sales multiple of ~12x by ascribing relative weightings to the 
multiple implied by the broader comp. set (Software + Data Analytics and government 
services) and the median multiple of new Space companies. We ascribe a 75% weighting to 
the ~15x multiple implied by the broader comp set and a 25% weighting to the median 
multiple (~3.1x) of the new Space peer group. We view this as justified to reflect PL’s 
structurally recurring revenue base, growing contribution from higher-value insight and 
monitoring solutions, and expanding portfolio of software-like satellite services, while 
acknowledging that hardware-enabled programs remain part of the business.

valuation.

PL continues to trade at a premium to Space peers, trading at ~11x 2027 EV/Sales (vs. 
Space median of ~3.1x). We note that short interest in PL remains elevated, with ~13% of 
the stock sold short.
