A&D Outlook for 2026 
The outlook for A&D remains mostly positive into 2026. This is especially true in Aerospace, given strong demand and a gradual supply increase that combine to yield 
visible growth. There is a similar dynamic in Defense, though the outlook is more nuanced, at least in the US, given changing industry dynamics and political uncertainty. 
Within Aero, we favor BA, SARO, and ATI, but still see potential in high fliers GE and HWM. In Defense, we lean toward LHX in products and we are downgrading LMT to 
Neutral. We also favor LDOS in services. Happy holidays!       
◼ Aero outlook remains quite positive . . . With multiple years of production in Boeing and Airbus backlogs, traffic growing MSD, and an aircraft fleet that is ~2 years 
older on avg than pre-pandemic, the backdrop for comm’l aero manufacturers is favorable. At times, the market creates a zero-sum dynamic between OE and AM, 
and eventually, rising aircraft production should boost retirements and dampen aftermarket growth; however, we do not expect this to play out in 2026 and believe 
both OE and AM can continue to prosper, with a significant backlog of engine maintenance work in particular.
◼ . . . And we favor BA, SARO, and ATI. BA remains a top pick and the opportunity is straightforward: the company must build and deliver more aircraft. 2026 cash 
flow expectations have come down, but there should be a visible path to at least $10b by decade-end and getting there means grinding it out through rate increases 
and improving Defense cash flow. SARO trades below 11x our 2027 adj EBITDA estimate, but engine maintenance demand is strong, SARO has new capacity 
coming online, margin rate should expand, and mgmt will have cash to deploy. We could see all of this contributing to re-rating and there is ~35% upside to our 12-
month target. ATI is wrapping 2025 on a high note following ups and downs, but we still see room to run, with multi-year A&D growth that should accelerate when 
destocking ends later this year. ATI trades at discount to peers and we think the market can increasingly come to appreciate the visibility here.
◼ 2025 was another banger for GE and HWM, but we remain constructive. In last year’s outlook, we noted that valuations were quite high for these two stocks . . . 
and yet they have each gone on to appreciate by ~80% YTD vs 17% for the S&P 500. Some of this reflects higher valuations and P/Es north of 40x on 2026 induce 
a bit of vertigo; nevertheless, the scale of multiple expansion is to some degree a function of larger estimate increases for these two companies. We see potential for 
more beats and raises, which keep us OW despite multiple years of outperformance. This past year, GE showed the potential for Aftermarket to grow well in excess 
of departures and while the difference is unlikely to be so stark going forward, we see Services growth remaining robust. HWM should benefit from capacity 
additions for both aero engines and IGT and we like the fact that HWM can benefit from both OE and AM demand.
◼ Defense has clear thematic tailwinds . . . Global defense spending is on the upswing as the US pulls back from commitments to defend allies who must now 
spend more to protect themselves, especially from Russia (in Europe) and from China (in Asia). Allies in these regions are doing more to develop their own defense 
industrial capabilities, but this takes time, and so US contractors have plenty of export opportunities. This new approach has not, however, resulted in a “peace 
dividend” for the US, with the administration keen to spend on homeland defense (Golden Dome) and on deterring peer competitors (China). This resulted in a 
reconciliation bill with roughly half a year of additional investment acct spending.

◼ . . . But it can be tricky as well. Despite a growing market, investors must be a bit more discriminating in Defense. This administration favors expanding the 
industrial base beyond the traditional prime contractors and encouraging more company-funded investment, creating concerns about share loss and returns for 
larger companies. As a result, the market has embraced smaller and non-traditional defense stocks, which we see in the fact that KTOS is the best performing stock 
in our group YTD. The increased focus on non-traditional contractors makes sense, though it requires a different approach than defense investors are accustomed 
to, with less focus on traditional valuation metrics, a greater need to understand individual programs, and a smaller role for cash return. Defense stocks are also 
subject to changing and unpredictable US political landscape that includes increasingly irregular budgeting, gov’t shutdowns and award slowdowns, and an 
upcoming midterm election.
◼ We favor LHX among larger cap product companies; LDOS in services. We have 20% upside to our 12-month price target for LHX, which is the highest among 
our larger cap defense stocks. The company seems poised for solid MSD growth in 2026 with a bit of margin expansion. Aerojet’s recovery is progressing and we 
should see years of accelerated growth here while IMS seems underappreciated. Space is a key opportunity, though SDA awards have slipped and the LEO satellite 
space is competitive. Our main fundamental concern is domestic radio budgets, but there is visible growth on the international side in the near-to-mid term. In 
services, we highlight LDOS, where we see opportunity for growth and margin expansion in Defense Systems while expecting VBA profitability to hold up. Valuation 
is appealing at only 15x next year’s adj EPS and there will be cash to deploy.

Bull and Bear Cases for Commercial Aero 
◼ Aero remains an end market with structural growth 
◼ Large backlogs provide visibility for OEMs
◼ Aircraft production rates beginning to increase more 
consistently
◼ Older fleet, further shortfalls in new aircraft 
production can benefit aftermarket players
◼ Several players in the industry retain pricing power
◼ There is pent up demand for engine maintenance 
that can drive volume growth well ahead of ASKs
◼ There are no imminent plans to launch a new 
comm’l aircraft  
◼ Now that traffic has recovered, the industry is more  
exposed to potential demand shock (geopolitical, 
macro, etc)
◼ Supply chain challenges remain and can disrupt the 
ramp at any time
◼ A significant pick up in retirements can cut into 
growth and pricing power in the aftermarket  
◼ Several US aero stocks trade at exceptionally rich 
valuations by historical standards  
◼ Market may have grown complacent regarding aero 
prospects   
Bull Bear
◼ There is a zero-sum element in bull-bear points between OE and AM  
◼ But there are periods when both have done well: 2004-2007, for example

We’re Still Likely Years Away from New Comm’l NB Aircraft
◼ It is not unusual to hear chatter about a potential new aircraft but we don’t see most players being in a rush 
◼ Airbus and CFM seem most willing to explore this topic 
◼ Has has discussed a potential engine selection in 2027, program launch ~2030 and EIS post-2035  
◼ GE/CFM appear determined to push RISE, including open fan technology, to generate well over 20% fuel burn gain 
◼ RISE could bring less wear and tear on some engine parts, addressing durability issues around LEAP  
◼ RISE would likely require changes to airframe configuration that could offset some fuel burn benefits 
◼ Boeing is not rushing to bring forward a new aircraft 
◼ Mgmt does not believe market is ready, does not believe tech is ready, and does not believe company is ready
◼ Boeing has other certifications, production ramps, and defense programs to focus on, plus balance sheet repair 
◼ Pratt & Whitney is skeptical of open fan configuration 
◼ In the meantime, we expect press reports about potential new aircraft and the game theory around them
◼ It is not surprising for Boeing to talk to Rolls, as WSJ reported; they likely talk to others as well 
◼ Would Pratt and Rolls re-team for a next gen narrowbody? 
◼ A new aircraft cannot accommodate both an open fan like RISE and a traditional ducted fan
◼ We assume Boeing and Airbus will be reluctant to go sole source; what will they do if there is only one open fan?
◼ CFM plan seems to be selling operators on the benefits of RISE and dragging the rest of the industry along
