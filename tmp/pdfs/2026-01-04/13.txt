Fashion Coverage 2026 Outlook: RL Top Pick;
Also Buy TPR, M, PVH
We prioritize momentum-led upside to estimates vs. turnarounds at this point.
While we appreciate meaningful multiple expansion vs. history, we continue
to see value in RL and TPR, esp. NT. Comp turnaround, Bloomingdale's share
gains, and potentially cc rev provide favorable setup for M. Remain Buy on
PVH although remove from Franchise Picks. For Holds, we are more optimistic
in DECK 2H earnings upside and see VFC val. as already reflecting Vans
improvements.
Macro Environment and Valuation Framework:  We believe across our space the market is
expecting a more resilient consumer and constructive tariff backdrop at least for the near-term.
Our base case for '26 assumes stable consumer spend, although still pressured at the low-end.
We focus on cos with earnings momentum to drive further upside vs. turnarounds at this juncture.
Performance for our brands this year aligned well for those with w/ higher DTC exposure (DECK
a notable underperformer), one of the key reasons we continue to like RL and TPR. For 2026, we
group our coverage into four categories: 1) AUR-driven growth (RL, TPR), 2) sales stabilizations/
turnarounds (DECK, CPRI, VFC), 3) MT value (PVH, M, KSS), and 4) M&A integration (LUXE).
Updated Stock Views
Ralph Lauren - BUY (RL, $425 PT) - We expect co. to outperform w/ potential for HSD%+ rev growth
near-term, led by brand heat + structural AUR elevation. We expect a strong holiday qtr, supported by
improved QTD US alt data, to unlock higher earnings revisions (led coverage in '25) as co. proves the
value consumer is resilient/has elevated, key for more LO interest. An improving luxury backdrop,
balanced growth across regions and channels, mgmt. execution, and healthy B/S further support
our favorable 12-month outlook. $425 PT = ~24x P/E on FY27 EPS of $17.64 (+5% vs. Street). We
see reasonable bull case of $475. This is based on LDD% EPS upside to Street next yr, which at 25x
P/E would be 1.6x PEG, above apparel peers of 1.2x but well below luxury of 2.5x.
Tapestry - BUY (TPR, $142 PT)  - We see accel. alt data trends QTD (web and foot traffic, search
trends) and higher pricepoint innovation + lower promos = AUR driving Q2 upside. As a bull case, we
believe the market is underestimating the ability for Coach NA to grow 20%+ in Q2 and LDD% in the
2H (i.e. improved 2YR stacks). Attractive industry positioning as affordable luxury and a bullseye
focus on target gen z consumer should fuel growth NTM. A strong holiday Q2 while "comping the
comp", Kate Spade trough sales declines at -mid-teens%, and FY GM% inflection to positive through
tariff mitigation could boost sentiment. $142 PT = 21.5x P/E on FY27 EPS of $6.53 (+5% vs Street).
We see low-20xs P/E as a warranted premium during DD% growth phase in our view vs. ~16x peer
avg. We look to younger consumer health, Tabby franchise expansion, and potential resistance to
higher pricepoints as risks to monitor.

Macy's - BUY (M, $27 PT)  - While stock has had a meaningful run, we see continued upside, which should increasingly be driven by earnings
revisions and greater signs of sustainable positive comps into 2027 (we see this as possible post store closures). Co. guided Q4 conservatively despite
encouraging Q3 and QTD trends, which we believe creates an achievable NT setup. Bloomingdale's is a standout at +9% comp in Q3, highlighting
share gains, supportive luxury tailwinds, and benefits from a multi pricepoint portfolio. Tariff impacts have been better than expected, while core GM
% is also expanding. AUR grew ~5% in Q3, an accel. and supported partially in our view by tariff-related price increases. We see AUR momentum (20
consecutive quarters of growth), BD's strength, SG&A leverage from store closures, and potentially cc revs (outpacing currently at DD%) as upside
levers next yr to Street. Over the MT, we believe co. could look to increase dividend from current $0.73 (3% yield) closer to 2019 levels of $1.51. Net
leverage is healthy at 1.0x, excl. leases.
Deckers - HOLD (DECK, $102 PT) - We slightly raised our HOKA and UGG DTC Q3 estimates based on improving alt data, which leads to our Q3 UGG
sales estimate of +1%, above the Street's flat. We see potential for further NT upside depending on holiday demand and full-price sell through, while Q4
DTC compares also ease materially. While we are more positive on NT results exceeding expectations, we await more signs of sales stabilizing and
margin expansion before underwriting upside next year. The next Clifton update, the co's largest HOKA franchise, in Fall '26 could be key in improving
DTC momentum. We are also closely watching the evolution of lifestyle/other categories and wholesale expansion as catalysts. Mgmt. has typically
guided conservatively, and promos in the competitive space (mgmt has viewed promo levels as abnormally low) and tariff spillover could lead to
limited ability to grow margins next year (Street -50bps Y/Y). Our $102 PT represents 15x P/E, in range with peers despite historical outperformance
given slowing key DTC and US growth metrics. Tariff relief could provide outsized benefits given conservative guide, the need for LFL price increases,
and ability to buyback shares given strong balance sheet ($1.4B cash, no debt).
Capri - HOLD (CPRI, $24 PT)  - Sentiment has improved driven by core Michael Kors business showing traction w/ full-price selling and the Versace
sale, which enables substantially all debt paydown. We are encouraged by product and marketing changes at MK, along w/ store remodels, although
wait for improved execution on outlet and wholesale before underwriting upside to operating income estimates. Jimmy Choo innovation for handbags
and footwear appealing to a broader customer base could also support a return to growth. Market will be focused on mgmt's expectation for a return
to growth in 2H next year. We increased our NT MK estimate due to improving alt data and our out-year EPS estimate led by continued investment
hedge income and modest buyback. We view current valuation of ~15x P/E and ~11x EV/EBITDA as fair at current levels.
VFC Corp - HOLD (VFC, $18 PT) - With alt data trends improving and newness at Vans gaining some traction (e.g. Knu Skool, Super Lowpro), sentiment
has been upbeat. We expect the brand to show sequential improvements NT and slight positive inflection in 4Q27, slightly later than the Street's 3Q27.
We model Vans Q3 growth this year at -8.1% vs. -7.8% cons., with an improvement to -2.5% (vs. -2.9% cons.) in Q4, largely driven by easier comparisons
as co. laps channel rationalization initiatives from LY. While growth is expected to improve, Vans has posted a -HSD% decline in underlying growth
for four consecutive quarters. We look to more visibility on sales impacts from product innovation under new leadership (which can take up to ~18
months to see impact) and marketing to build conviction in a shorter timeline for recovery. That said, we see risk the co. looks to continue pruning
lower-tier distribution in the event newness lags further and upcoming price increases meet resistance. Co’s P/E valuation remains relatively elevated
at ~20x vs. 10-year avg. of 18x, which featured a strong Vans growth cycle. We believe estimate revisions will be the key driver of stock upside next
year as the co. enters “show-me” mode with a cleaner slate for Vans in FY27. Tariff mitigation and cost savings realization net of investments are a
potential meaningful upside lever to estimates, w/ the latter needed in our view to support MT upside.
Kohl's - HOLD (KSS, $24 PT)  - Recent quarter comps of -1.7% were the best since 3Q21, and we see the company well-positioned to capitalize on
PL demand, having right-sized its assortment over the past few quarters vs. nat'l brands as consumers become more choiceful and value-driven. PL
should be a margin tailwind, as every 100bps of penetration translates to 10-15bps of gross margin improvement, helping to offset tariff pressures
expected to intensify in Q4 and into 2026, even though the impact so far has been less than expected. Credit card rev remains a notable opportunity and
could be a source of upside, with recent signs of stabilization and improved spending trends. We are looking for more consistent execution as co. works
to stabilize comps next year and improve its digital presence, which is already showing progress. Current net leverage stands at 1.2x (excl. leases), with
$500M of debt maturing in 2031 remaining a focus point by investors. Sentiment remains relatively negative, w/ short interest still elevated at ~26%.
LuxeExperience - HOLD (LUXE, $9 PT) - FY26 is a transition year for co. as it executes post-YNAP transformation. Successful and faster than expected
integration of YNAP Luxury, given SG&A is >10ppts above legacy MYTE, should drive cost reductions and operational synergies, which we believe
could be the primary driver of upside to estimates next year. By segment, mgmt. forecasts MYTE growth +MSD% to +HSD%, well below the 15-20%
MT CAGR target. YNAP Luxury is expected to decline -LSD%, a significant improvement from -LDD%, while YOOX to see -DD% from declines of >20%
as it resets its rev base. Early signs of progress at YNAP Luxury are visible, with margin improvements in Q1 supported by higher full-price selling, and
mgmt. expects YNAP Luxury to return to growth in 2H26. While val is cheap on EV/Sales at ~0.3x and broader luxury tailwinds should provide support,
we believe market needs further signs of execution before considering becoming more constructive.

Company Description
Capri Holdings
Capri Holdings Limited, a global fashion luxury group, consists of three brands: Michael Kors, Jimmy Choo, and Versace. These brands cover multiple
fashion luxury categories, including women’s and men’s accessories, footwear and ready-to-wear, as well as wearable technology, watches, jewelry,

Deckers Outdoor

Kohl's Corporation
Based in Menomonee Falls, Wis., Kohl's is a family-focused, value-oriented specialty off-mall department store offering moderately priced, exclusive
and national brand apparel, shoes, accessories, beauty and home products in an exciting shopping environment. The company operates over 1,100
stores in 49 states.
LuxExperience

Mytheresa in April 2025. Segments include Mytheresa luxury, YNAP luxury, and YNAP off-price.
Macy's
Macy’s is a national retailer headquartered in Cincinnati, Ohio, operating over 6 00 stores under the Macy’s, Bloomingdale’s, and Bluemercury name-
plates.
PVH
PVH is a leading global fashion company with the vast majority of sales through its two iconic brands in Tommy Hilfiger and Calvin Klein.
Ralph Lauren
Ralph Lauren is a leading global fashion brand with offerings across apparel, footwear, accessories, home, fragrances, and hospitality.
Tapestry, Inc.
Tapestry is a designer and retailer of handbags, footwear, and accessories in North America and internationally. The brand portfolio includes Coach
and Kate Spade. Its products are sold through company-operated retail and factory stores in North America, Japan, China, as well as its online store
and catalogs and through department store locations in the U.S., internationally, and at specialty retailers.
VF Corporation
V.F. Corporation, founded in 1899 and headquartered in Denver, CO, is a global producer of apparel, footwear, and accessories brands across
the outdoor, active, and workwear segments. VFC's portfolio includes brands such as Vans, The North Face, Timberland, and Dickies. In FY23,
approximately 45% of the company's sales were DTC, with the remainder being wholesale.
