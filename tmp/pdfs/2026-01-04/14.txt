 What’s in store for 2026? 
As 2026 approaches, we debate the three key questions that we see shaping each sub- sector for 
the year ahead. We expect the European retail environment to remain fairly low -growth, while the 
sourcing and currency outlook  looks more benign. In this context , valuation multiples appear to be 
more of the opportunity than major earnings corrections, and several stocks trade on materially lower 
P/Es than historically. Our top picks are M&S and Zalando (see also A retail top pick for 2026), two 
stocks with good sales momentum that we think offer potential for margin expansion and multiple re-
rating. We reiterate our Underperform ratings on H&M and Kingfisher. 
Sporting Goods: knocked down, get up again 
After a challenging 2025, we examine whether there are positive catalysts in 2026 for Adidas, Puma 
and JD Sports. We continue to favour Adidas (+), which appears to have the most self-help in 2026.  
Apparel retail: mind the multiple gap 
Apparel demand may stay muted and sourcing benign in 2026. The bigger debate revolves around 
valuation. Marks & Spencer (+) becomes a top pick, and we still favour Inditex (+) over H&M (-). 
Online retail: separating winners & losers 
EU de minimis removal, rising fulfilment volumes, and Zalando’s (+) AI-driven data advantage make 
it a top pick. The path to return to top-line growth is less clear for ASOS (-) and Boohoo (-).   
Hardlines retail: stock picking 
Our UK Household Cash Flow suggests c.+1% spending growth in 2026, making stock-picking key. 
We favour Currys (+) over Kingfisher (-) and see an attractive risk-reward for a B&M (+) re-rating.

Executive Summary 
What’s in store for 2026? 
As 2026 approaches, we debate the three key questions that we see shaping each sub-
sector for the year ahead.  
In sporting goods, we continue to back Adidas (+), where valuation looks undemanding 
and where we bridge 2026 revenues to show anticipated support from the World Cup. 
It’s too soon to become constructive on Puma (=) or JD Sports (=), in our view. 
In apparel retail, we question the multiples in the sector rather than consensus earnings 
expectations. In our view, the multiple gap between Inditex (+) and H&M ( -) is not wide 
enough, while the gap between Next (=) and M&S (+) could close. M&S is a top pick. 
In online retail, Shein was less disruptive in 2025 than we expected, and de minimis rule 
changes may require it to adjust its business model. Online players continue to sweat 
their logistics assets, and top pick Zalando (+) looks best placed for margin expansion.  
In hardline retail, our UK household cashflow model suggests c.+1% spending growth. 
Self-help will be key, and we favour Currys (+) and B&M (+) over Kingfisher (-).

Sporting Goods: knocked down, get up again 
2025 was challenging for the European Sporting Goods stocks. The key question is 
whether there are positive catalysts in 2026 for Adidas, Puma and JD Sports to drive 
better share performance. In our view, Adidas’ valuation multiple is attractive, given the 
brand’s momentum. We think the other two stocks require greater external assistance. 
The main catalyst for Puma could be corporate activity, while JD Sports may require 
better momentum among US consumers and from Nike if the stock is to re-rate. 
2025 highlights 
Brand momentum dominated 2025, with Adidas maintaining strong momentum against 
market fears o f slowdown, while Puma’s momentum slowed sharply and a new 
management team has reset the business. The market backdrop was quite challenging, 
particularly with US tariffs, with sales at the market leader Nike declining and strong 
growth among smaller brands. In this environment, most stocks struggled.  
Three questions for 2026 
 
[1] Can Adidas shares work as the turnaround moves into the later stages? 
Adidas (+) shares have fallen 32% since the start of 2025. The stock’s 12-month forward 
P/E multiple has fallen from c.31x to c.15x in that time. No doubt investors had hoped for 
a continued beat-and-raise pattern, and while consensus 2025 EPS fell by c.1% and 
consensus 2026 EPS by 4% through the course of the year  (albeit a fall of 8.5% from 
February’s peak), the halving of the P/E multiple still seems unduly harsh in our opinion.

Adidas’ sales recovery began in 2024 and continued throughout 2025. Initial momentum 
in footwear has broadened out into apparel, and growth has been healthy both in lifestyle 
and performance. We think that the low -profile and terrace trends will remain relevant 
during 2026, Adidas should scale other footwear franchises through the year, and the 
FIFA World Cup s hould add a revenue opportunity. Management has suggested that 
gross World Cup revenues should be EUR 1bn from Q4 2025 onwards, and we assume 
a net sales benefit in 2026 of EUR c.560m. This means that it contributes 230bps to our 
2026 annual constant currency sales growth forecast of +9.5%.

Although we have trimmed our Adidas estimates, as we discuss in more detail in the 
company section of this report, we expect it to achieve 2026 EPS of EUR c.11. This does 
not factor in any support from share buybacks, which are an upside risk  given balance 
sheet strength. On this basis the s tock currently trades on a CY26 P/E of around 15x, 
materially below the 24x that the stock traded at in the 5 years before Covid (or 19x in 
the decade before the pandemic). Hence in our view the downside risk sits more with 
earnings than with the P/E multiple, whereas in 2025 the risk was to the multiple but there 
was limited risk to EPS due to brand momentum. But given the lowest P/E multiple in 
around a decade, we see upside risk in the year ahead. 
 
[2] Is there a catalyst at Puma in 2026? 
Puma (=) shares had an extremely challenging year in 2025, down >50% YTD , with 
management change and a strategy re-set that pushed the company into losses. 
Under new CEO Arthur Hoeld’s leadership, Puma expects 2026 to be a ‘transition year’. 
We view it as the early stage of a long and difficult brand turnaround. Inventory is 
expected to peak in Q4 2025 after the company takes back inventory from its wholesale 
partners. As management indicated in a chart which we reproduce in Figure 10, it had 
allowed mass merchants to become too important in its revenue mix, which over time 
has impacted brand strength. The company has not indicated specifically which retailers 
these may refer to, but on the basis that it has called out the US as the market where 
distribution is the greatest problem, it may refer to retail partners such as TJX, Costco, 
Ross Stores or Burlington Stores. We model Puma’s adjusted EBIT margins recovering 
to a modest 1.2% in 2026, helped by license income from United Legwear.

There is no guarantee that Puma’s turnaround efforts will be a success. The 
extraordinary turnaround at Adidas since 2023 was something of a historical outlier due 
to some unique product trends and competitive landscape circumstances. Most 
turnarounds take time in sporting goods: examples like Under Armour and Reebok show 
this.

In early 2026, perhaps at the Q4/FY results on 26 February, Puma’s management is 
expected to set out its guidance for 2026 and ambitions for the medium -term. It has 
already said that by 2027 it expects to achieve profitable sales growth, ahead of the 
market growth rate. A measure of success could be that by the middle of 2026 its 
wholesale partners are commenting that Puma has made good progress cleaning up its 
distribution, which Puma has committed to completing by the end of 2026, and 
commenting that its 2027 Spring/Summer ranges are showing a marked improvement. 
This would be the first season that new management can materially influence.

TikTok Shop launched in France and Germany earlier this year

LLM 
Large Language Models such as ChatGPT and Google Gemini 
typically target shoppers who already know what they want to buy, 
not those seeking inspiration. Unlike TikTok Shop, LLM does not 
yet facilitate the end-to-end transaction.  
 
One key capability that LLMs lack is the inspiration-driven, 
personalised experience that third party aggregators like ASOS 
and Zalando have embedded into their platforms, albeit to varying 
degrees. 
ASOS: recently introduced its AI Assistant and AI Stylist that 
suggest outfits and styling tips, becoming increasingly 
personalised as customers interact with it.

initiatives like Stories by Zalando, Digital Fashion Assistant and 
Outfit Generator for the past 2 years. Its unique data advantage – 
over 61 million active customers – provides a rich foundation for 
training and personalising AI models.  
 
Agentic AI 
Agentic AI makes cross-platform price comparison clearer and 
simpler. A shopper can ask the AI to find the lowest price for a 
specific brand across multiple sites, a hassle-free way to locate 
the best deal. Like with LLM, this targets shoppers who already 
know what they want to buy. 
Marketplaces usually cannot set prices for branded products – the 
brand owner retains pricing authority to safeguard its image. 
However, typically the less established brands under a wholesale 
model, with low brand heat, gives marketplaces pricing control, 
allowing for variations in price.  
Similarly, both LLMs and Agentic AI lack the inspiration and 
discovery functions that third-party aggregators like ASOS and

Explanation of Research Ratings 
Rating System  
Our coverage analysts use a rating system in which they rate stocks as Outperform, Neutral, or Underperform (as defined below) relative to other companies 
covered by the analyst or the analyst’s team, over a 12-month investment horizon. 
Prior to 23 September 2024, BNPP ratings were relative ratings defined against the performance of the relevant MSCI sector index, at either the country or 
regional level over a 12-month investment horizon. 
Outperform (O/P): The stock is expected to outperform the average total return of all companies covered by the analyst or the analyst’s team, over a 12-
month investment horizon. 
Neutral: The stock is expected to perform in line with the average total return of all companies covered by the analyst or the analyst’s team, over a 12-month 
investment horizon. 
Underperform (U/P): The stock is expected to underperform the average total return of all companies covered by the analyst or the analyst’s team, over a 
12-month investment horizon. 
Under review: The rating of the stock has been placed under review after significant news. Any possible change will be confirmed as soon as possible in 
the form of a new broadly disseminated report. 
Restricted (RS): The stock is covered by BNPP but there is no Rating and no Target Price because BNPP is involved in an equity capital market/ merger 
and acquisition transaction relating to the subject company.  
Not Rated (NR): The stock is covered by BNPP but there is no Rating and no Target Price at this time. 
Not Covered (NC): BNPP does not cover this company.

or downside to target price relative to the current share price determined the recommendation. TEB Investment now uses a relative recommendation 
structure whereby the applied rating is based on the stock’s expected performance as compared to the average total return of all companies covered by the 
analyst or the analyst’s team in Turkish stock coverage over a 12-month investment horizon. TEB Investment research reports with ratings and target prices

ESG Rating Explanation and Methodology 
ESG integration methodology: The BNP Paribas approach offers an alternative to mechanistic ESG scores and leverages the in-depth knowledge of our 
industry equity research teams by combining quantitative and qualitative factors. There are three steps to our framework; firstly, our teams build a materiality 
map to assess which ESG topics are most relevant to their respective industries. Secondly, for the chosen topics, companies are assessed relative to sector 
peers. The assessment can be based on metrics and qualitative judgements. Each company is given a one to five score per topic, with five being the best. 
Thirdly, based on the topic scores, and any other significant ESG factors, we identify companies in the sector as ESG Leaders, ESG Laggards or ESG 
Average. The definition of the ratings is shown below; they are not based on the average topic score as some topic scores can be more material than others. 
ESG Leader: Relative to sector peers an ESG Leader is better positioned on the chosen ESG topics, or other relevant ESG considerations. 
ESG Average: A company rated as ESG Average may have strong or poor performance on an individual ESG topic, but overall has an average exposure 
to ESG risks and opportunities. 
ESG Laggard: Relative to sector peers an ESG Laggard is poorly positioned on the chosen ESG topics, or other relevant ESG considerations for the stock.
