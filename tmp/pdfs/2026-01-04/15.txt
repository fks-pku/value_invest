European laundry –  how bad can it be? 
 
4 DECEMBER 2025 
Securities Research Report 
Production time: 06:07* (London time) 
Research Analysts & Publishing Entities 
 
 European Laundry Detergents (c.6% of Henkel’s sales) seem to be one of investors’ key concerns 
into next year – and for good reason: with the absence of notable input cost pressures and consumer 
sentiment relatively subdued, the pricing backdrop will likely be tough. With this in mind, we analyse 
20 years of Nielsen retail sales data looking for clues as to how things may evolve. Ultimately, while 
the category will likely be deflationary, we think pricing is unlikely to collapse. We maintain O/P. 
Latest consumer confidence reads do not point to further material private label pressures 
While recent years have seen unprecedented private label share gains (c.340bp in 2022 -23, stable 
since), this was driven by the sharpest -ever drop in European consumer confidence (the Russia -
Ukraine war and resulting inflation). Despite the recent consumer confidence r eads being far from 
stellar, they do not point to material further PL pressures. We also note that brands can snap back: 
branded players’ shares tend to recover (at least partially) when consumer confidence rebounds. 
The category will probably be deflationary, but pricing is unlikely to collapse 
The oil price is one of the key determinants of European Laundry Detergents ’ pricing; with this in 
mind, 2026 is likely to be deflationary. However, looking at the oil price trajectory, the pressure should 
be nowhere near as material as it was in 2016/17, when the industry saw the two worst pricing prints 
in the past 20 years ( -2.3%/-1.2%). Also ( and perhaps stating the obvious), it is a two- way street 
when it comes to elasticities in laundry – investing in price usually leads to volume improvement. 
Despite muted European laundry outlook, cons. for Consumer Brands looks achievable 
Our analysis of this key area of investor concern leads us to conclude that BBG  cons. expectation 
for HCB FY26 LFL at +2.4% is achievable: muted European laundry trends should be at least partially 
offset by easy comps elsewhere. While this pace of growth is hardly exciting and is below Henkel’s 
mid- to long-term ambition of +3-4%, it should provide comfort that the worst seems to be passing in 
HCB. Coupling this with v. depressed valuation, we continue to see attraction in the shares.

European laundry – how bad can it be? 
Introduction 
Reflecting on our recent investor interactions, looking into 2026, European laundry is one 
of the key investor concerns wrt. Henkel –  and for good reason: with the absence of 
notable input cost pressures and consumer sentiment relatively subdued, the pricing 
backdrop will likely be tough. Consider the following comments made by Henkel wrt. its 
European Laundry & Home Care business with the recent sales update (see 
Q325 sales 
and 15 questions for management): 
– “In Europe… Consumer Brands recorded a negative development reflecting the 
continued challenging market environment, particularly in Laundry & Home Care, while 
Hair showed good growth.” 
– “[Consumer Brands] pricing was slightly negative, particularly due to Laundry & Home 
Care, which also reflects the currently challenging environment with regards to consumer 
sentiment, while pricing in Hair was in positive territory… Laundry Care was negative due 
to Fabric Cleaning, while Fabric Care delivered double-digit growth.” 
– “[In] Q3, why is the pricing lower? It is particularly more related to Laundry, so not to 
all categories. Within our portfolio, Home, Hair (be it consum er, be it professional) is in 
the territory of neutral to positive when it comes to pricing. So, in that context, this is more 
Laundry related. And within Laundry, it is also more Europe related. … after… a little bit 
better consumer sentiment in Q2, the consumer sentiment especially turned down again 
in Q3. And that is also related to the consumer behaviour. And in Europe you know that 
private labels are more outspoken… And on top, the market overall in laundry was 
negative, not related to Henkel but in the overall context.” 
– “…you know that the Laundry segment is currently facing a more pronounced 
competitive environment. Also… the market overall is negative in the context of the 
consumer sentiment, which is impacting consumer behaviour, which is also a more trend 
in the direction to private-label -oriented brands. But from my point of view, as I always 
pointed that out, that ’s a temporary development. If sentiment comes back, I would 
[expect to]  significantly see then also a shift again towards the branded labelled 
businesses.” 
– “[On private label pressures], as indicated before, it is predominantly in Europe. The 
private label share in Europe is around 20% and that’s something which you don’t see in 
any other region in the part when it comes to Laundry.”

for Henkel, it is not as material within the group context as the number of the above 
comments may lead one to think: European Laundry Detergents accounted for est. 
c.13% of Consumer Brands and est. c.6% of Henkel’s group sales in FY24 (see the figure

Conclusion 
Our analysis of this  key area of investor concern leads us to conclude that Bloomberg 
consensus’ expectation for Consumer Brands FY26 LFL at +2.4% is reasonable: muted 
European laundry trends should be at least partially offset by some Henkel-specific (and 
not only Henkel-specific) headwinds in the base, including the following:  
1) De-stocking: The magnitude of destocking weighing on the US trends in H125 was 
rather pronounced by historical standards (see US destocking: here to stay? and US 
channel shift: how do individual stocks screen?). 
2) Supply chain:  With its H1 results, H enkel commented that the supply chain 
challenges had been resolved by the beginning of Q2, thus having only a minor spillover 
effect on Q2 growth. 
3) Portfolio measures: These have now been largely completed, with the sold Retailer 
Brands business in North Am erica (annual sales of ~EUR500m) having been 
deconsolidated from 1st April 2025; Henkel had commented that the business was dilutive 
to growth and margins.

Explanation of Research Ratings 
Rating System  
Our coverage analysts use a rating system in which they rate stocks as Outperform, Neutral, or Underperform (as defined below) relative to other companies 
covered by the analyst or the analyst’s team, over a 12-month investment horizon. 
Prior to 23 September 2024, BNPP ratings were relative ratings defined against the performance of the relevant MSCI sector index, at either the country or 
regional level over a 12-month investment horizon. 
Outperform (O/P): The stock is expected to outperform the average total return of all companies covered by the analyst or the analyst’s team, over a 12-
month investment horizon. 
Neutral: The stock is expected to perform in line with the average total return of all companies covered by the analyst or the analyst’s team, over a 12-month 
investment horizon. 
Underperform (U/P): The stock is expected to underperform the average total return of all companies covered by the analyst or the analyst’s team, over a 
12-month investment horizon. 
Under review: The rating of the stock has been placed under review after significant news. Any possible change will be confirmed as soon as possible in 
the form of a new broadly disseminated report. 
Restricted (RS): The stock is covered by BNPP but there is no Rating and no Target Price because BNPP is involved in an equity capital market/ merger 
and acquisition transaction relating to the subject company.  
Not Rated (NR): The stock is covered by BNPP but there is no Rating and no Target Price at this time. 
Not Covered (NC): BNPP does not cover this company. 
 
As of 23 September 2024, TEB Investment has changed the recommendation rating structure for Turkish stock coverage from Buy, Hold and Reduce to 
Outperform, Neutral and Underperform. Ratings and target prices for dates prior to 23 September 2024 used an absolute rating structure whereby the upside 
or downside to target price relative to the current share price determined the recommendation. TEB Investment now uses a relative recommendation 
structure whereby the applied rating is based on the stock’s expected performance as compared to the average total return of all companies covered by the 
analyst or the analyst’s team in Turkish stock coverage over a 12-month investment horizon. TEB Investment research reports with ratings and target prices 
for dates prior to 11 August 2025 were prepared and distributed without involvement of a FINRA member firm. 
ESG Rating Explanation and Methodology 
ESG integration methodology: The BNP Paribas approach offers an alternative to mechanistic ESG scores and leverages the in-depth knowledge of our 
industry equity research teams by combining quantitative and qualitative factors. There are three steps to our framework; firstly, our teams build a materiality 
map to assess which ESG topics are most relevant to their respective industries. Secondly, for the chosen topics, companies are assessed relative to sector 
peers. The assessment can be based on metrics and qualitative judgements. Each company is given a one to five score per topic, with five being the best. 
Thirdly, based on the topic scores, and any other significant ESG factors, we identify companies in the sector as ESG Leaders, ESG Laggards or ESG 
Average. The definition of the ratings is shown below; they are not based on the average topic score as some topic scores can be more material than others. 
ESG Leader: Relative to sector peers an ESG Leader is better positioned on the chosen ESG topics, or other relevant ESG considerations. 
ESG Average: A company rated as ESG Average may have strong or poor performance on an individual ESG topic, but overall has an average exposure 
to ESG risks and opportunities. 
ESG Laggard: Relative to sector peers an ESG Laggard is poorly positioned on the chosen ESG topics, or other relevant ESG considerations for the stock.
